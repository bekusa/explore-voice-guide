The guide

The story

Listen for a moment. Under the music drifting from hidden speakers, you can hear the scrape of strollers, a dozen languages, and the soft crunch of shoes on paving stones. The air carries caramel, steamed buns, and the faint green smell of trimmed hedges. Ahead, a castle rises above the trees, its blue roofs catching the Shanghai light. You are standing in Shanghai Disneyland, on the eastern edge of the city in the Pudong district. This park looks familiar at first glance, but it was built to feel different.

Shanghai Disneyland opened in June 2016. It was the first Disney park in mainland China, and it came after years of planning and negotiation. The resort is a joint venture between The Walt Disney Company and a state-owned Shanghai company. That partnership shaped the place in quiet ways. Disney's designers spoke often about making a park that was authentically Disney and distinctly Chinese. As you walk, try to notice where those two ideas meet. Sometimes the join is obvious. Sometimes you have to look closely.

Here is the first surprise. Most classic Disney parks welcome you with Main Street, an old-fashioned American small town with shopfronts and a railway station. Shanghai skipped it. Instead, the entrance leads onto Mickey Avenue, a shorter street of cartoon-style facades built around Mickey Mouse and his friends. The designers decided that a nostalgic American main street would mean little to most visitors here. So the welcome is about the characters themselves, not about an American memory of the past.

Beyond Mickey Avenue lie the Gardens of Imagination. This is where the Chinese layer of the park is easiest to see. Garden design has deep roots in Chinese culture, where a garden is a place for strolling, resting, and looking at carefully framed views. These gardens take that idea and fill it with Disney stories. There are flowers, open lawns, a carousel, and the flying elephant Dumbo turning slowly in circles. Families spread out here between rides, and the pace drops.

Look for the Garden of the Twelve Friends. The Chinese zodiac has twelve animals, one for each year in a twelve-year cycle. Each animal is matched with a Disney or Pixar character and shown as a large mosaic. Many visitors walk straight past without stopping. Local families do the opposite. They search for their own birth year and take a photo beside it. Ask a Chinese friend which animal they are... it is one of the easiest conversations to start in this park.

Now turn toward the castle. It is called the Enchanted Storybook Castle, and it is the largest castle in any Disney park. Older Disney castles each belong to one princess, such as Sleeping Beauty or Cinderella. This one was designed to represent all the Disney princesses together. That is the second surprise. Look at the roofline and you may spot a golden peony at the top. The peony is a flower with strong meaning in China, linked to wealth and honour.

The castle is not only something to look at. You can walk inside it. There is an attraction that takes you through the castle on a gentle storybook journey, a restaurant within its walls, and a walkway that leads through the building itself. In the evening, the castle becomes a screen. Lights, projections, and fireworks turn its walls into a moving picture. Crowds gather early for a good view, and the whole park seems to turn and face the same direction.

Head toward the rocky peaks of Adventure Isle. Here the theme is an imagined lost world, with waterfalls, rope bridges, and a towering mountain called Roaring Mountain. The stonework is shaped and painted to look worn by weather and time. Listen for rushing water and the shouts from rafts as they spin through the rapids. This land feels more rugged than the gardens. Paths narrow, rocks close in, and the sounds of the central plaza fade behind you.

Next door is Treasure Cove, the first Disney land built entirely around pirates. Its centrepiece is a Pirates of the Caribbean ride that differs from the older versions in America and Europe. Instead of drifting past scenes in a slow boat, riders are carried into a large underwater battle built with screens, sets, and moving boats. Notice the details as you queue — weathered wood, rope, lanterns, and the smell of damp stone. Even fans who know the older rides find this one new.

On the far side of the park, Tomorrowland looks toward the future. Its most famous ride is TRON Lightcycle Power Run, which first opened here before a version came to Florida. Riders sit astride motorbike-style vehicles and launch at speed under a curving glowing canopy. At night, that canopy shines in blue and white, and it is one of the strongest shapes in the park. Even if you do not ride, walk beneath its edge after dark. The light changes the mood completely.

The park keeps growing. Toy Story Land opened a couple of years after the park, filling a corner with oversized toys and bright colours. Later came Zootopia, a land based on the animated city of animals, which opened in late 2023. Its streets are built to the scale of different creatures, so look for doors and windows of very different sizes. Watch how the planners keep adding lands. A Disney park is never finished, only paused between projects.

The resort reaches beyond the park gates. Outside, there is a shopping and dining area called Disneytown, hotels, and a large park with a lake that visitors can stroll around for no ride at all. A metro line connects the resort to central Shanghai, so many visitors arrive by train. Leave a little time for the walk out. The lakeside paths are calmer than the park, and they show how much of the site is landscape rather than ride.

Before you move on, stop for a moment wherever you are standing. Look at the castle roofs, the hedges, the people around you. Notice the small things — a child holding a balloon, a grandparent resting on a bench, a cast member waving at a passing family. This park was built for those moments as much as for its rides. As you walk away, listen again to the music and the many languages in the air. They are part of the design too.

Before you go

Practical tips

Take the metro to the Disney Resort station.
Claim a castle viewing spot early for evening shows.
Ride TRON again after dark for the glowing canopy.
Rest in the Gardens of Imagination between busy rides.
Save time for a quiet walk around the lake.
