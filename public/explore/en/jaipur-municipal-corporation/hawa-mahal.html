The guide

The story

Stand on the street below and look up. A great pink wall rises high above you. Hundreds of tiny windows cover its face. They catch the soft light of the morning. A gentle wind moves through the stone. This is Hawa Mahal, the Palace of Winds. It is one of the best loved sights in the city of Jaipur.

The palace was finished in 1799. It stands in the old heart of Jaipur, in the region of Rajasthan, in India. A ruler named Maharaja Sawai Pratap Singh ordered it built. He was the grandson of the king who founded the city. The design came from a builder called Lal Chand Ustad. Together they made something light and strange and beautiful.

The front of the palace has five floors. From the street, it looks like a tall honeycomb of windows. It also looks like a crown. Many people say the shape honors Krishna, a beloved Hindu god. The wall is thin and open. It feels more like a carved screen than a heavy fort. It seems almost too delicate to stand.

The windows give the palace its name. There are 953 of them. Each small window is called a jharokha. Fine stone screens cover each one. The screens are carved into careful patterns. Wind passes through them all day long. The moving air cools the rooms inside, even in the hot months. That is why people call this the Palace of Winds.

The windows also had a quiet purpose. In that time, royal women lived apart from public life. They did not show their faces to strangers. This custom was called purdah. From behind the screens, the women could look down at the street. They watched festivals, markets, and wedding marches. No one below could see them. The palace gave them a way to share the life of the city.

Picture those days for a moment. A procession moves slowly down the street. Music rises into the air. Bright colors fill the road. Behind the carved windows, the women of the court lean close to watch. The screens hide their faces. Yet they see everything that passes below. The whole street becomes their stage.

The palace is built from sandstone. The stone is red and soft pink. This warm color matches the rest of old Jaipur. Long ago, the city was painted in this tone to welcome a royal guest. The color stayed. Today people call Jaipur the Pink City. Hawa Mahal is one of its most famous faces.

Step inside, and the building surprises you. There are no grand staircases. Instead, long smooth ramps lead from floor to floor. The rooms are small and simple. The upper floors grow narrow. The whole structure is more like a tall, thin wall than a deep palace. It was made to be looked through.

Light is the real treasure here. In the early morning, the sun strikes the front wall straight on. The pink stone begins to glow. The many windows throw soft shadows across the surface. Some windows hold colored glass. The light passes through and scatters across the floors. The plain rooms fill with quiet color.

Walk around to the back, and you find another surprise. The famous front is only one face of the building. From behind, you see plain walls, open ramps, and small courtyards. The grand screen was always meant to face the street. It works like a stage, designed to be seen from one side only.

Climb to the upper floors and look out. From there you can see across the whole old city. Rooftops spread in every direction. Temple towers rise among the houses. Beyond them stand the dry hills. On one high ridge sits an old fort, watching over the town. The palace was a window onto all of this.

There is a calm here that the crowds cannot break. The stone has held the same shape for more than two hundred years. Wind still moves through the screens. Light still climbs the wall each morning. The building does the same quiet work it has always done. Crowds come and go below. Cameras rise and fall. But the wall itself stays the same, hour after hour, season after season.

Hawa Mahal was built for watching. For over two centuries, it has stood above the street, patient and still. Once the court looked down at the city from here. Now the city looks up at the palace. Stand a moment in its shade. Feel the wind pass through the old stone, the same wind that gave the palace its name.

Key facts
