The story

On the island of museums, a great nineteenth-century museum rebuilt from its own war ruins holds the treasures of ancient Egypt, among them one of the most famous faces in the world. This is the Neues Museum, home to a painted bust of a queen that draws visitors from across the globe.

The museum was built in the middle of the nineteenth century, badly destroyed in the war, and then left a ruin for decades before a celebrated modern restoration brought it back, weaving new construction into the surviving scarred walls.

That restoration is itself part of the experience: rather than hide the damage, it preserves the bullet-marked brick and broken surfaces alongside new work, so the building openly carries its own history of destruction and recovery.

The collection's star is the painted bust of an ancient Egyptian queen, more than three thousand years old, her serene, elegant face one of the most reproduced images of the ancient world, displayed alone in a place of honour.

Around her spread the museum's wider Egyptian and prehistoric collections, with sculpture, papyrus, mummies, and artefacts that trace the ancient civilisations of the Nile and early Europe.

The bust is shown in a hushed, dim room of its own, and photography of it is not allowed, so visitors come face to face with the queen herself rather than through a screen, a quietly powerful encounter.

The museum is part of the great cluster on the island, recognised together as a World Heritage Site, so it pairs naturally with the neighbouring collections for those with time.

The blend of ancient treasure and modern restoration gives the museum a particular character, the very old and the carefully new held together in one building by the river.

As one of the most popular museums on the island, it is busy, so booking a timed ticket ahead helps, and an early visit gives the famous bust to you with fewer crowds.

Beyond the star attraction, the museum rewards a slower look at its prehistoric and Egyptian halls, and at the restored architecture itself, which is admired as a model of how to rebuild a ruined monument.

Stand before the painted queen in her quiet room, then take in the scarred and mended walls that hold her, and the museum's double story, ancient art and modern recovery, comes together. When you step back out onto the island, the other great museums wait along the river.
