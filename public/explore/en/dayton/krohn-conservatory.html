Krohn Conservatory

Push open the door, and the air changes at once.

⏱ 5 min
▶BeginOpen the Lokali app

The story

Push open the door, and the air changes at once. It turns warm and wet against your skin. Green fills every direction that you look. Water runs somewhere close by, out of sight. Glass curves high overhead, holding in the heat. You have stepped out of the changing Ohio weather and into a warm living greenhouse.

This is Krohn Conservatory, set in Eden Park. It was built in 1933. The frame is made of aluminum and glass, in the clean modern style of its time. The clear walls let in the sun from every side at once. Inside, under all that glass, plants from the warm parts of the world grow well all year long.

The building carries a name worth knowing. It honors Irwin M. Krohn, who served on the city's park board for many years. His steady work helped protect green spaces just like this one. The conservatory has kept his name since the 1930s, through many turning seasons and many hundreds of thousands of visitors who have walked through.

Walk forward into the main palm house. Tall palms rise toward the high glass roof. Their wide green leaves spread out far above your head. The narrow path winds between thick growth and pale hanging flowers. The warm air stays heavy and close around you. Each slow breath carries the smell of damp soil and growing things.

Listen now for the water. Inside the rainforest room, a waterfall falls about twenty feet over dark wet rock. The steady sound fills the whole space, soft and constant. Ferns grow thick and green in the rising spray. The moving water keeps the air damp and close, just as it would inside a real tropical forest far away.

The conservatory holds a large living collection. More than three thousand five hundred kinds of plant grow here, gathered from all around the world. Some come from dry open deserts. Some come from deep green jungle. Under one long glass roof, climates that lie far apart on the map sit only a few short steps from each other. Few buildings in the city let you cross the whole warm world so quickly on foot.

Move now into the desert house. The air turns dry and light at once. Here the plants are spare, slow, and tough. Cactus and aloe hold water inside their thick green bodies. Some are round, some are tall, and some are covered all over in fine pale spines. They show how life shapes itself to survive on very little.

Look closely at the orchids. Their flowers take strange and careful forms. Some hang down in long heavy sprays. Some sit alone on a single thin stem. The colors range from clean white to deep velvet purple. Each bloom carries its own fine pattern, shaped slowly over time to draw a certain insect close to it.

Each spring, the conservatory holds a butterfly show. Live butterflies fly free among the plants and flowers. They land on petals, on leaves, and sometimes even on visitors. The whole warm room fills with small moving color. Children stand very still and patient, hoping a butterfly will choose to come and rest on their open hand.

Find a bench among the leaves and sit. The glasshouse is a calm and easy place to rest. Outside, the weather may be cold or grey or wet. Inside, it stays always green and warm and still. Many people come here in the depth of winter, just to feel the heat and to see living color again.

Notice the glass and the fine metal frame above you. It is light and thin, built to hold as much sun as it possibly can. In 1933 this counted as a modern way to build. The structure still does its quiet patient work today, gathering light for the plants below, season after season after season.

Walk the full loop once more, slowly, before the door. Feel the air shift from wet to dry and back again. Each room is its own small world, sealed under the same shared roof. The whole building is a careful machine for growing, holding many far climates gently together in one calm and humid place.

Step back outside into the open air of Eden Park. The temperature shifts sharply against your warm skin. The smell of wet soil stays with you for a moment longer. Behind you, the glass house holds its warm green world, steady through every season, ready to welcome you back in from the cold.

Key facts

Built in 1933 within Eden Park, Cincinnati.
Named for parks commissioner Irwin M. Krohn.
Holds more than three thousand five hundred plant types.
A rainforest waterfall drops about twenty feet inside.
A live butterfly show returns here each spring.

What to look for

Tall palms reaching up toward the glass roof.
The twenty-foot waterfall in the rainforest room.
Thick water-storing bodies of the desert cactus.
Strange careful shapes of the hanging orchids.

Practical tips

Visit in winter to escape the cold outside.
Come during spring for the live butterfly show.
Pass from the warm house to the dry room.
Rest on a bench among the green leaves.
