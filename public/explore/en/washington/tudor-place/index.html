The story

Behind a garden wall in the quiet upper streets of the old quarter stands an elegant early-nineteenth-century mansion, its rooms filled with the possessions of one family across nearly two centuries. This is Tudor Place, a neoclassical house and garden that offers a rare, intimate look at the long life of a single household.

The house was built in the early nineteenth century for a granddaughter of the country's founding first family, and it remained in the hands of the same family for some six generations, an unusually long single ownership.

Because one family kept it for so long, the house is filled with their accumulated belongings, including pieces passed down from the founding family, so it is a remarkable, continuous record of how the wealthy lived across the centuries.

The mansion itself is a fine example of the neoclassical style of its day, with a distinctive domed and columned garden front, designed by a noted architect of the early city.

Around it spreads a beautiful garden, much of it laid out long ago, with old trees, box hedges, flower beds, and quiet corners, one of the loveliest private gardens in the area, now open to the public.

Inside, the rooms are shown much as the family kept them, with their furniture, portraits, and everyday objects, so a visit feels less like a museum and more like stepping into a lived-in family home.

The continuity is the special thing: where many historic houses show a single moment, this one shows the slow accumulation of a family's life, change layered on change under one roof.

The house is visited by guided tour, which tells the story of the family and the objects, while the garden can often be wandered more freely, so the two together fill a calm, rewarding hour or two.

It sits in the quiet residential heights of the quarter, above the busy shopping streets, so it is peaceful and rarely crowded, a retreat into the past away from the bustle.

For anyone interested in how the early elite of the country lived, and in the long thread of a single family, it is one of the most rewarding houses in the city.

Walk the rooms full of one family's long history, then out into the old garden, and the slow, continuous life of the house surrounds you. When you step back out onto the quiet street, the busy shopping quarter waits a short walk downhill.
