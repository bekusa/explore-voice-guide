The guide

The story

A long drive opens in front of you. Fountains line both sides and catch the sun. At the end stands a great building the colour of sand and gold. Domes rise above it, large and small. Palm trees sway in the warm wind off the sea. The scale is hard to believe. This is the Emirates Palace.

This is not an old palace, though it looks like one. It is a hotel. It opened in 2005. The government of Abu Dhabi built it. The aim was clear. They wanted a place that would show the wealth and pride of the young nation. They also wanted a stage for great events. Kings and leaders have met here many times.

Come closer and look at the domes. The largest one rises high above the central hall. Around it sit many smaller domes. Their colour shifts from cream to soft gold. The walls below are pale, like desert sand. The whole building seems to grow out of the land and the light. At sunset, the gold tones deepen and glow.

Step into the main hall. Look up into the great dome above you. Gold spreads across the ceiling in fine patterns. Light from high windows pours down. Below, the floor shines with polished stone. The space is huge, yet warm. Your voice seems to rise into the dome and soften. Many visitors simply stand and turn in a slow circle.

Gold is everywhere here, used with a free hand. It runs along the ceilings and the walls. It edges the doors and the high arches. Thin sheets of real gold leaf cover many surfaces. The builders did not hide their wish to impress. Yet the patterns follow old Arab and Islamic designs. The wealth is wrapped in tradition, not set against it.

The hotel is famous for one small pleasure. You can order a cappuccino topped with flakes of real gold. The gold has no taste. It is there for the eye and for the story. Sit for a while with the cup in your hand. Around you, staff move quietly across the marble. For a moment, you share in the life of the palace.

Walk through the long halls and you will lose your sense of size. Corridors stretch far into the distance. Grand staircases curve upward. Crystal lamps hang in rows. Fresh flowers fill tall vases. Everything is kept in perfect order. The hotel was made to feel endless, and it nearly does.

Find your way out to the gardens at the back. They are wide and green, which is rare in this dry land. Paths wind between palms and lawns. Water keeps the air cool. Beyond the gardens lies a private beach. The sand is pale and soft. The Gulf spreads out, flat and bright, to the far line of the sky.

From the beach, turn and look back at the building. It rises above the green like a city of domes. The sea breeze carries the smell of salt and flowers. Boats rest at a nearby marina. This meeting of desert, garden, and sea is the heart of the place. It shows what care and water can make.

Through the years, the Emirates Palace has welcomed the world. It has hosted summits where leaders sign agreements. It has held concerts, shows, and grand dinners. Its management has changed over time, but its role has not. It remains a public face of Abu Dhabi. Even guests who do not stay the night come to look.

It is fair to ask what such a place means. Some see only great wealth on display. Others see pride, and a wish to be taken seriously. The truth holds both. The palace was built to say that Abu Dhabi had arrived. Standing in its halls, it is hard to argue with the message.

Before you leave, step back onto the long drive. Watch the fountains rise and fall in the light. A building like this is a kind of statement, written in stone and gold. Whatever you make of it, it asks to be remembered. Carry your own thought about it as you go.
