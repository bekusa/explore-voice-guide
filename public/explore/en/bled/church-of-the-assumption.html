The guide

The story

The boat touches the landing, and a stairway rises in front of you, straight up into the trees. At the top, a bell tower shows white above the leaves. Somewhere inside, a rope waits. The lake laps softly against the stone behind you, and the boatman holds the dock while you step off.

This small island is the only one in Slovenia, and a church has stood on it for a very long time. The church is given to the Assumption of Mary. People have crossed the water to worship here across many centuries, long before the building you see today took its present shape above the trees.

From the landing, a stone stairway climbs to the church door. There are ninety-nine steps. By an old custom, a groom carries his bride up all of them before their wedding, while she stays silent the whole way. Couples still try it today, to laughter and cheering from the people watching at the bottom.

The church you see now took form in the seventeenth century, in the full Baroque style of that age. But it stands on much older ground. Beneath it lie the traces of earlier churches, Gothic and Romanesque, each one raised in turn upon the ruins of the church that stood before it.

Older stories reach back further still. Long before the church, people say a temple to a goddess once stood on this same island, in the time before these lands turned to the Christian faith. No one can show you its walls now. The tale survives only as a tale, passed down by word from far back.

Step inside. The room is small but richly worked. Gold catches the light on the altar. Painted figures look down from the walls and the curved ceiling above. After the bright lake outside, the dimness takes a moment to settle, and then, slowly, the detail and the color appear all around you.

At the center hangs the rope of the bell. This is the part most visitors come for. You take the rope in both hands and pull, and high in the tower above the bell answers, full and clear. By tradition, you may make a wish as it rings. Many people do, and the line for it is patient. Children pull hardest of all, and the bell forgives them.

A story is tied to that bell. People tell of a widow who once lived by the lake, grieving a husband lost to the water. She gathered her silver and had a bell cast in his memory. As it was carried across the lake, a storm rose, and the bell sank to the bottom, where some say it rings still on the quietest nights.

The story goes on. The widow, in her sorrow, left for Rome and gave the rest of her days to the church. When she died, the Pope heard of her grief, had a new bell made, and sent it to be blessed and hung on the island. That is the bell, people say, whose rope you hold today. Whether the tale is true, no record can prove. It is offered as a story, and a fine one.

Behind the altar, a worn patch of older painting survives from a time before the Baroque work began. It shows that this room has been changed and repainted again and again, each generation leaving its own mark upon the walls, none of them quite able to erase the hand that came before.

Outside again, walk the short path around the island. It takes only a few minutes. Old trees lean out over the water. A few low buildings stand near the church, including a house for the clergy and a small place to rest and drink. From the edge, the castle is visible, high on its distant cliff across the lake.

Time your visit for a quieter hour if you can. In the middle of the day the boats arrive together, and the small church fills quickly with people and voices. Early or late, the island calms. The steps empty. The bell, when it rings, carries clean and alone across the surface of the water.

Before you go back down, stand a moment at the top of the steps. The lake spreads out on every side, ringed by mountains. The rope is warm where many hands have held it. Pull it once, listen, and let the sound travel out over the water toward the peaks, and then begin your slow way down. Each step down opens a new view of the water.
