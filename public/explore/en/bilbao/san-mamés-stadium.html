San Mamés Stadium

A roar rises from somewhere inside the walls.

⏱ 5 min
▶BeginOpen the Lokali app

The story

A roar rises from somewhere inside the walls. Even out on the street, you can feel it in your chest. Thousands of voices lift and join as one. This is San Mamés, the home of Athletic Club. On match days, the whole district moves to its rhythm. Red and white scarves fill the pavements all around. The air smells of grilled food from the stalls.

Football has been played on this spot for a long time. The first San Mamés opened in 1913. For a hundred years, the club played its home games there. Over time, the fans gave the old ground a nickname. They called it La Catedral, the cathedral of football. The name showed just how much the place meant to them. Few grounds carry such a proud name.

The years slowly wore the old stadium down. So the club built a new one in its place. The new San Mamés opened in 2013. It first rose beside the old ground. Then the old stands came down, and the new building took over the site. The name stayed the same. The spirit simply moved across with it. Older fans still tell stories of the first cathedral.

Look at the outside of the building. It is wrapped in panels that twist gently along its curve. At night, the panels glow with light. The colors can be changed for each game. Sometimes the whole stadium shines in red and white, the colors of the club. You can see it glowing from right across the river. On a dark night, it looks like a lantern by the water.

Inside, the stands rise steeply on all four sides. This design brings the crowd in very close to the pitch. The sound has almost nowhere to escape. It stays inside the bowl, and it grows. Visiting teams often speak of the wall of noise that meets them here. For the home side, that noise is a real help. Players say the ground seems to push them forward.

The club that plays here is unusual in the world of football. Athletic Club fields only local players. They come from the Basque Country and the lands nearby. Most other big clubs buy talent from around the world. This club, instead, builds its team from its own region. The fans take deep pride in this old rule. Young players come up through the club's own training side.

The team is known as Los Leones, the Lions. The name is linked to the stadium itself. San Mamés is named for a young saint named Mammes. An old story tells how he was sent to face hungry lions. In the story, the lions chose not to harm him. From that tale, the club took the lion as its symbol. You will see lions in the badge and around the ground.

Walk around the ground on a quiet day. You may see fans stop to pose by the gates. Scarves and shirts hang in the shop windows nearby. The streets carry the names of old players and famous wins. Small plaques and murals mark great days from the past. Memory lives in the small details all around the stadium.

The club runs a museum inside the building. It tells the story of more than a hundred years of play. Cups, medals, and old shirts sit behind glass. Faded photographs line the walls. You can follow the team from its first days right up to the present. It is a good place to begin a visit. Allow yourself an hour to take it all in.

On many days, you can also take a tour. You walk out through the players' tunnel. You sit in the dugout right beside the pitch. You stand on the steps where the loudest fans stand. From the empty stands, the field looks wide, green, and very still. The silence feels strange in such a large place.

When a match ends, the crowd flows out into the streets. Talk turns at once to every pass and every goal. People gather in the nearby bars to go over it all. The result will be discussed for days to come. Win or lose, the club remains the pride of the whole city. That pride does not depend on the score.

Stand near the stadium as the lights begin to dim. The day's great noise has faded away now. The huge curved shell stands quiet beside the river. It seems to rest, and to wait. It waits for the next match, and the next roar, as it has done for so many years.

Key facts

The first San Mamés opened in 1913.
Fans nicknamed the old ground La Catedral.
The current stadium opened in 2013.
Athletic Club fields only Basque and local players.
The team is known as Los Leones, the Lions.
