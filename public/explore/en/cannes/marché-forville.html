The guide

The story

The smell reaches you first. Ripe peaches, fresh basil, cut flowers, the sea-salt tang of fish on ice. Voices rise and fall under a high roof. Crates of fruit spill color across the tables. You have entered the Marche Forville, the covered market of Cannes. Traders call out prices. The air is cool here, even when the street outside is warm.

The market sits just behind the old port, at the foot of the old town. It has stood here for generations, serving the people of Cannes. The roof is open at the sides, so air moves through freely. Light falls between the supports onto the stalls below. In the morning, the place is full. By early afternoon, the traders begin to pack away.

The stalls follow the seasons. In spring come strawberries and young vegetables. Summer brings tomatoes, melons, and peaches. Autumn fills the tables with mushrooms, grapes, and figs. Winter offers oranges and dark greens. Much of the produce comes from farms in the hills behind the coast. The growers themselves often stand behind the tables.

The fish stalls sit toward one side. Ice glitters under whole fish and shells. The catch comes from the Mediterranean, brought in that same day where possible. Cooks from the town's restaurants come early to choose the best. You can watch a trader clean and cut a fish in a few quick strokes. The skill comes from years of practice.

There are flowers here too. Buckets hold roses, lilies, and bright local blooms. The cut-flower trade has long been part of this coast. Fields of flowers once covered the nearby hills. Some still do. The scent mixes with fruit and herbs into something you will remember. Many shoppers leave with a paper-wrapped bunch under one arm.

Cheese and cured meats fill another corner. Rounds of goat cheese sit beside hard mountain wheels. Sausages hang in rows. The sellers offer small tastes on the tip of a knife. Olives shine in wide bowls, green and black, dressed with herbs and oil. This is food made the way it has been made in this region for a very long time.

One day stands apart from the rest. On most mornings the market sells food. But on Mondays, the tables hold old goods instead. Traders bring furniture, plates, books, and odd treasures. This is the brocante, the flea market. People come to hunt for a small find. If you visit on a Monday, expect dust and history, not fruit.

The market is a place to watch as much as to buy. Local people greet the traders by name. They test a melon by its smell. They argue gently over the ripeness of a tomato. These small exchanges repeat every morning. To stand and watch them is to see the ordinary life of the town, away from the seafront hotels.

If you want to taste the region, this is the place to gather a meal. Buy bread, cheese, fruit, and olives. Add a handful of tomatoes and some cured ham. Carry it all to the port or the beach. A market lunch costs little and tastes of the place itself. You need no restaurant to eat well in Cannes.

The traders are used to visitors now. Many speak a few words of several languages. A smile and a slow question will carry you far. Point if you cannot name a thing. Ask for a small amount if you are unsure. Most sellers would rather you taste and return than buy too much and not come back.

Try to come early. The first hours hold the freshest goods and the fullest tables. By noon the choice narrows. The traders are tired and ready to close. Bring a cloth bag if you can. Bring small coins and notes. Some stalls take cards now, but cash is still quicker and more welcome at many tables.

When you leave, step back into the street and the light. The cool of the market stays with you for a moment. So does the smell of basil and peach. Markets like this one are old and plain. They feed the town day after day. In a place known for glamour, this is where its daily life still beats.
