The guide

The story

Look up. That is the first thing this building asks of you. It rises from the centre of Durban in pale stone, wide and tall, topped by a great dome against the sky. This is the Durban City Hall. Stand back across the street to take it in. The sound of traffic moves around you, but the building itself seems calm and still above it all.

The City Hall was completed in 1910. It was built in a grand style that looks back to the architecture of much earlier centuries, full of columns, arches, and carved stone. The design closely follows the City Hall in Belfast, far away in the north of Ireland. The two buildings are near twins, one in the cool north, the other under the warm Durban sun.

Walk slowly along the front of the building. Tall columns line the entrance. Above them, carved figures stand in stone. These figures stand for things like art, music, and learning. Look closely and you can pick out the shapes of instruments and books held in their hands. The stone has weathered over the years, but the detail is still clear.

The great dome rises high above the centre. From the street it seems to float over the roof. Smaller domes and towers stand at the corners. The whole front is balanced and even, one side matching the other. It was made to look solid and lasting, a sign that the young city meant to endure.

Step inside, and the air turns cool and quiet. The entrance hall is wide, with a floor of patterned stone. A staircase climbs toward the upper floors. Light falls from high windows. Footsteps echo softly off the hard surfaces. After the heat and noise outside, the calm within comes as a relief.

The City Hall is not only a place of offices. It holds a museum, an art gallery, and a library, all under one roof. The Natural Science Museum fills part of the building with displays of animals, insects, and bones. Among them is the model of a long-extinct bird and other creatures from the deep past. Children move from case to case, pointing.

On another floor is the Durban Art Gallery. Its rooms hold paintings, sculpture, and craft, both old and new. The gallery was among the first in the country to collect the work of local Black artists in a serious way. The walls hold many styles and many voices, gathered slowly over a long span of years.

The grand hall inside is used for concerts and ceremonies. It has a high ceiling and rows of seats facing a stage. A large pipe organ stands at the front. When it is played, the sound fills the whole room, deep and full. Many of the city's important events have taken place within these walls.

Step back outside and cross to the open square in front. From here you can see the whole face of the building at once. People pass through the square all day. Some sit on the steps to rest. Pigeons gather and scatter. The City Hall watches over the daily life of the city centre, as it has for more than a hundred years.

Around the building, the streets of central Durban spread out in every direction. Shops, offices, and older buildings stand close together. The City Hall sits among them as a kind of anchor. It marks the heart of the old town, the point from which the rest of the centre seems to be arranged.

What stays with you is the ambition of the place. A city barely a few decades old chose to build something this large and this fine. The people who raised it wanted Durban to be taken seriously, to stand among the great cities. In stone and dome and column, they wrote that wish down for everyone who came after.

Come back in the evening if you can. As the sun drops low, the pale stone turns warm and golden. Lights come on one by one along the front of the building. The carved figures and the high dome stand out against the darkening sky. The square grows quieter as the shops begin to close. In the soft evening light the City Hall looks older and grander still, less like a place of offices and more like a monument. It is worth seeing the same building twice, once by day and once by night.

Before you move on, stand once more before the entrance and look up at the dome. It has watched the city change beneath it for generation after generation. The crowds, the cars, and the shopfronts have all shifted many times. The dome has not. It simply waits, quiet and sure, for the next person to stop and look up.
