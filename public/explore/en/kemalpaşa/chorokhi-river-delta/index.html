The story

Listen first. Before you even see the river, you hear it, the soft rush of fresh water meeting salt, and above it, the high, sharp cries of gulls. The Chorokhi River ends its long journey here, spreading out into the Black Sea just south of Batumi, close to the old stone walls of Gonio fortress.

The Chorokhi is a great traveler. It begins high in the mountains of northeastern Turkey, where it is called the Çoruh, and runs fast and cold through deep, narrow valleys. By the time it reaches this coast, it has crossed the border into Georgia and slowed down, grown wide and heavy, ready at last to rest in the sea.

A river delta is the place where a river finally loosens its grip. Instead of one strong channel, the water splits into many smaller ones. It drops the sand and stones it has carried for hundreds of kilometers. Here, those deposits form low gravel banks, shallow pools, and shifting islands that change their shape with every season and every flood.

Stand at the edge and look down at the ground beneath your feet. The stones are smooth and rounded, polished by their long ride downstream. Some are pale, some dark, all worn soft by water and time. Walk carefully here, because the gravel is loose, the footing shifts, and the channels can be deeper and faster than they look from the bank.

This is one of the best places near Batumi to watch birds, and birdwatchers come here often. The mix of fresh water, salt water, open gravel, and quiet pools draws many kinds. Gulls and terns wheel and call overhead. Herons stand still as statues in the shallows, waiting. Small wading birds run along the water's edge on quick legs, probing the soft mud for hidden insects and worms.

The timing matters a great deal. In spring and autumn, this coast becomes a highway for migrating birds. The whole Batumi area is known to birdwatchers around the world for the great rivers of raptors, hawks, eagles, and buzzards, that pass overhead in autumn, riding the warm air south. The delta below offers them water and a quiet place to rest before the next stage of the journey.

Bring patience, and if you have them, binoculars. Birds do not perform on demand, and the delta rewards those who wait. Sit quietly on the bank, let your eyes adjust to the distance, and slowly the still landscape begins to fill with movement. A flash of white, a long curved beak, a sudden lift of many wings at once, and then quiet again, as if nothing moved.

The delta feels wilder than the city beaches a short way north. There are no rows of umbrellas here, no music, no crowds, only the open land and water. The wind comes straight off the sea. Tall reeds bend and hiss along the channels. The mountains stand behind you, and the water stretches flat and wide in front, all the way to the flat, grey horizon.

To the south rises Gonio, where an ancient Roman fortress once guarded this very river mouth. The soldiers stationed there chose the spot for good reason. Whoever held the delta controlled the route inland along the Chorokhi valley, deep toward the mountains. The quiet water you are watching has shaped the movement of armies and traders for around two thousand years, since the time of Rome.

The light here changes quickly. Clouds roll in off the sea, then break apart again. The river's surface turns silver, then grey, then silver once more. On clear evenings, the sun drops straight down into the water at the horizon, and the wet gravel along the banks shines with the color of the whole sky, gold and pink and deep blue.

Take care with the place itself. This is a living system, and fragile in its own way. The nesting birds, the loose banks, and the plants that hold them all together are easily disturbed. Move slowly and softly, keep your distance from resting flocks, and leave the stones, the reeds, and the nests exactly as you found them.

A delta is an ending and a beginning at the very same time. The river you see has finished its long fall from the mountains, and yet its water now joins the wider sea and begins all over again. Stand on the gravel as the gulls settle down for the night, and you can feel that slow, patient handover of fresh water to salt.

Key facts

The Chorokhi rises in the mountains of northeastern Turkey.
On the Turkish side the river is called the Çoruh.
The delta meets the sea just south of Batumi.
The Batumi area is famous for autumn raptor migration.
A Roman fortress at Gonio guarded the river mouth.

What to look for

Smooth river stones polished by the long journey.
Herons standing still in the shallow pools.
Channels splitting and rejoining across the gravel.
Tall reeds bending along the water's edge.

Practical tips

Bring binoculars to spot birds across the gravel flats.
Time your visit for spring or autumn.
Wear sturdy shoes; the loose gravel shifts underfoot.
Keep your distance from resting flocks of birds.
