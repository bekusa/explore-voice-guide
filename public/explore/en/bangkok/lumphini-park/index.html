Lumphini Park

The story

In the middle of the modern business district, where glass towers crowd the sky, a wide green park opens out around a lake, with joggers on the paths, families on the grass, and monitor lizards basking at the water's edge. This is Lumphini Park, the great green lung of central Bangkok and a welcome escape from the heat and traffic.

The park was created in the 1920s on what was then the edge of the city, named after the birthplace of the Buddha, and given to the public as a place of recreation. As Bangkok grew up around it, it became an island of green in an ever-denser city.

At its heart lies a large lake, where you can hire pedal boats shaped like swans and drift among the fountains. The water draws birds, fish, and turtles, and walking or paddling around it is the simplest pleasure the park offers.

The park's most surprising residents are large monitor lizards, which live around the lake and can grow startlingly big. Harmless if left alone, they bask on the banks and swim in the water, and spotting one is a small thrill for first-time visitors.

Lumphini is the city's outdoor gym. Early in the morning and in the cool of the evening it fills with people jogging, cycling, practising tai chi, dancing in groups to music, and using the open-air exercise machines, a daily ritual that is fascinating to watch.

The best time to come is early, soon after dawn, when the air is cool, the light is soft, and the park is full of locals exercising before work. Open-air aerobics classes and slow tai chi in the morning haze are a side of the city few visitors see.

Through the hot middle of the day the park is quieter, its trees offering shade and its paths a calmer route than the surrounding streets. It is a fine place to rest, read, or picnic away from the relentless pace outside.

The contrast with the city is the whole point. Skyscrapers rise on every side beyond the trees, so the park frames the modern city even as it offers relief from it, and the meeting of green and glass makes for striking views and photographs.

Beyond the lizards, the park holds many trees and birds, and quiet corners where you can forget the city for a while. It is a reminder that even a dense, hot megacity needs and keeps its green spaces.

The park hosts open-air events, from outdoor concerts in the cool season to markets and festivals, and it remains above all a place where ordinary Bangkok comes to relax, far from any tourist trail.

A few practical notes. The park is free and open from early morning to evening; come at dawn or dusk for the coolest air and the liveliest scene. Keep a respectful distance from the lizards, and bring water. It sits right by a transit station, easy to reach.

Find a bench by the lake, watch the joggers, the dancers, and the basking lizards, with the towers rising beyond the trees, and let the pace drop for a while. Lumphini is where the great hot city comes up for air. When you step back out onto the busy street, the calm of the park lingers a few minutes longer.
