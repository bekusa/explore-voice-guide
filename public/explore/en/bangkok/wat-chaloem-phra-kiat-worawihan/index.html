Wat Chaloem Phra Kiat Worawihan

The story

On the bank of the river toward the northern edge of the city, a graceful old temple stands among trees, its halls decorated with delicate coloured tilework and its white chedi rising above the water. This is Wat Chaloem Phra Kiat Worawihan, a royal riverside temple away from the crowds, known for its fine ornament and its peaceful setting.

The temple dates from the first half of the nineteenth century, built under a king of that era, and it carries the dignity of a royal foundation, with carefully made halls and a quiet, well-tended compound by the water.

Its decoration is its great distinction. The buildings are ornamented with delicate ceramic tilework in the Chinese-influenced style fashionable in its day, when trade with China shaped Thai art, giving the halls a refined, colourful surface unlike the plainer local temples.

A white chedi, a bell-shaped relic tower, rises within the compound, pale and elegant against the sky and the river, the temple's landmark from the water and a focus for the calm of the grounds.

The riverside setting is part of the appeal. The temple sits right by the water, so it was traditionally approached by boat, and arriving across the river still gives the finest first sight of its halls and chedi among the trees.

Because it lies away from the central sights, toward the city's northern edge, it sees few foreign visitors, so it offers a calm, local experience of a fine old temple rather than a crowded tourist stop.

The halls hold Buddha images and quiet shrines, and the compound, with its trees and riverside air, is a restful place to walk, a world away from the heat and bustle of the central temples.

As a working temple, it serves its surrounding community, with monks and the steady rhythm of merit-making and prayer, so a visit is also a glimpse of everyday religious life beyond the tourist trail.

The surrounding district, on the quieter reaches of the river, has its own old neighbourhoods, markets, and temples, so the temple can be part of a gentle exploration of a less-visited stretch of the waterway.

Reaching it is part of the experience: a trip up the river by boat, away from the busy central stretch, passing temples, houses, and gardens along the banks, is the natural and most pleasant way to come.

A few practical notes. Entry is generally free or a small donation; dress modestly, with shoulders and knees covered, and remove your shoes in the halls. Come in the cooler hours, ideally by river, and combine it with the quieter northern waterway.

Walk the calm riverside compound, admire the delicate tilework and the white chedi, and take in a fine old temple that few visitors reach. Wat Chaloem Phra Kiat rewards the trip up the river with quiet and craft. When you take the boat back toward the centre, the busy heart of the city feels far away.
