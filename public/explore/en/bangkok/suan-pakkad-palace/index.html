The story

Behind a wall on a busy modern street, a group of traditional teak houses stands among a green garden, hung with art and antiques, an unexpected pocket of old Thailand among the towers. This is Suan Pakkad Palace, a former royal residence turned museum, one of the city's quieter and more charming collections.

The name means, roughly, the cabbage garden, a memory of the market gardens that once stood on the site, and the palace was the home of a prince and princess who gathered here a fine collection of Thai art and antiquities.

Rather than a single grand building, the palace is a cluster of traditional teak houses, several of them old structures brought from elsewhere and reassembled in the garden, raised on posts in the classic style, around lawns and ponds.

Inside the houses, the family displayed their collection: Buddhist images, ceramics, carved furniture, musical instruments, shells, and antiquities, arranged in a domestic setting rather than a formal museum, so you see how a cultured household lived among its treasures.

The jewel of the collection is a lacquer pavilion, an old building with walls covered inside in fine gold-and-black lacquer painting, showing detailed scenes, one of the loveliest examples of the craft in the city.

The garden itself is part of the pleasure, a green, shaded retreat of lawns, trees, and ponds in the middle of the modern city, a calm space that feels far from the traffic just beyond the wall.

The collection ranges from ancient artefacts, including prehistoric pottery and bronze, through to fine Thai religious and decorative art, giving a broad sense of the country's long artistic history in an intimate setting.

Because it is small, personal, and off the main tourist trail, the palace is usually quiet, so it makes a calm, cultured pause from the heat and crowds, rewarding visitors who enjoy art and old houses.

It sits in a central, modern district near the elevated railway, so despite its hidden, old-world feel it is easy to reach, an unexpected oasis a short walk from the towers and traffic.

The combination of traditional architecture, a fine private collection, and a green garden makes it a rounded introduction to Thai art and domestic life, on a human, manageable scale.

A few practical notes. Entry is ticketed; dress respectfully and remove shoes to enter the houses, and allow an unhurried hour or two. It is reachable on foot from a nearby elevated railway station.

Walk the teak houses among the family's collected treasures, then sit in the green garden away from the traffic, and a gentler, older Thailand comes quietly into view. Suan Pakkad is one of the city's calm surprises. When you step back out to the busy street, the towers seem louder than before.
