The story

You hear the water before you see it. A steady roar grows louder as you walk, and then a cool damp touches your face. Green closes in around the path. This is Düden, where a river falls through rock and forest on its long way down to the sea.

There are in fact two waterfalls here, and they lie some distance apart. The upper falls are north of Antalya, in a wooded park. The lower falls are at the coast, where the same river drops off a cliff straight into the Mediterranean. Most visitors come to one or the other, and many never learn there are two.

Start at the upper falls. The Düden River runs through the park and then plunges into a deep basin below. What makes this place unusual is that a path leads behind the falling water itself. A natural cave curves under the rock, and you can walk into it, with the river pouring down in front of you.

Inside the cave the world changes. The water becomes a moving curtain of white between you and the daylight. The sound fills the whole space. Cool air pushes past you, and a fine mist settles on your skin and your clothes. It is several degrees colder here than out in the warm park outside.

Stand and look out through the falling water. The light comes through it broken and shifting, never still. Spray catches the sun in small flashes. The rock underfoot is wet and dark, so step with care as you move. People have stood in this exact spot, behind this same falling water, for a very long time.

Outside again, the park spreads along the river. Tall trees give cool shade. Paths cross the water on small bridges, and the river runs fast and clear between them. There are tea gardens where you can sit close to the steady sound of the falls. Ducks rest and feed in the calmer pools at the edges.

The water itself has traveled underground to reach this place. The Düden River runs partly through caves and channels beneath the land before rising again to the surface. This is limestone country, where rivers slip below the ground and return. The upper falls mark one point where the hidden water comes back into the daylight.

The lower falls are a different experience entirely. To see them, go to the coast on the eastern side of the city, near the Lara district. Here the river reaches the edge of a cliff and falls about forty meters straight down into the sea. There is no river mouth, no slow meeting of fresh water and salt, only the sudden drop.

A clifftop park looks out over the falls and the open water. From the edge you see the white column of water meeting the waves far below. On windy days the spray drifts back up the cliff face and dampens the air around you. The sea beyond stretches flat and wide to the horizon.

The best view of the lower falls is from the water. Boat trips run along this stretch of coast, and from the sea you can look straight at the cliff as the river pours over it. From that angle the scale becomes clear at last: the height of the rock, the force of the fall, the spray rising off the moving waves.

Bring something to keep you dry. At the upper falls the cave path leaves you damp with mist, and the stone is slippery underfoot in places. Flat shoes with good grip are worth having. The deep shade and the cool air also make this a fine place to come during the strongest heat of the day.

On a hot weekend the upper park fills with families from the city. They come to escape the heat, to share a picnic under the trees, and to feel the cool air that hangs close to the water. Children run back and forth across the bridges. Older men play cards in the shade of the tea gardens. Here the falls are part of daily life, not only a sight for visitors.

Whichever falls you choose, the meaning of the place is the same. Water moves through this land in a way you can stand inside and watch with your own eyes. It rises up from underground, falls through the forest, and gives itself at last to the open sea. Stay long enough to feel the mist settle, and the sound stays with you long after you leave.

Bring a light layer for the cool, misty cave.
See the lower falls by boat for the best view.
Visit midday in summer for shade and cool air.
