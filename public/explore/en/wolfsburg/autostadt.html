The guide

The story

Listen for a moment. Water laps against the bank of a wide, straight canal. Gravel crunches under your shoes, and somewhere nearby a car door closes with a soft, heavy thud. Ahead of you, two towers of glass and steel rise from the ground, filled from top to bottom with brand-new cars. Behind them, across the water, four tall brick chimneys stand over one of the largest car factories in the world. Welcome to Autostadt, the car city of Wolfsburg.

Before we walk further, it helps to understand where you are. Wolfsburg is a young town by European standards. It was founded in 1938 for one purpose: to build a small, affordable car for ordinary Germans. That car later became known around the world as the Beetle. The city and the factory grew up together, and in many ways they still breathe together. Autostadt sits right beside that factory, on the banks of the Mittelland Canal, a major inland waterway that crosses northern Germany.

Autostadt opened in 2000, the same summer that the world exhibition, Expo 2000, took place in nearby Hanover. Volkswagen built it as something unusual — part theme park, part museum, part landscaped garden, and part delivery centre. The idea was simple but bold. Instead of collecting a new car at a dealership, customers could come here, spend a day exploring, and then drive away in their own vehicle. Many still do exactly that.

That brings us to the two glass towers you have probably noticed already. These are the car towers, and they are the beating heart of the delivery process. Each tower rises around twenty storeys and holds hundreds of new cars, stacked neatly on platforms. Here is the surprise most visitors miss. The towers are linked to the factory by an underground tunnel. Freshly finished cars travel from the production line straight into the towers, without ever touching a public road.

When a customer arrives to collect a car, an automated lift glides up inside the tower, picks out the right vehicle, and brings it down. From the outside, you can watch the arms move through the glass, slow and precise. For many families, this moment is a real occasion. Parents bring children, couples take photographs, and some people travel a long way just to stand here and receive their keys. Watch the faces near the handover area — the excitement is genuine.

Now turn your attention to the ground itself. Autostadt is not only buildings. It is a carefully shaped park, with grassy hills, quiet lagoons, bridges, and paths that curve between the pavilions. The landscape designers wanted the site to feel calm and open, a contrast to the noise and speed of the factory next door. In spring and summer the lawns are green and soft. In winter, the site often hosts seasonal events, and the water and lights change the mood completely.

Scattered across this park are the brand pavilions. The Volkswagen Group owns many car makers, and several of them have their own building here. Each pavilion is designed to express the character of its brand. Some feel sleek and technical, others warm and elegant, others bold and dramatic. You do not need to be a car enthusiast to enjoy them. Treat them almost like small art installations, and notice how architecture, light, and sound are used to tell a story about a company.

If you love history, look for the ZeitHaus, which means time house. This is the car museum of Autostadt, and it is not limited to Volkswagen. Inside, you can trace the story of the automobile across many decades and many manufacturers. Early machines that look like carriages without horses sit not far from cars that once filled the streets of your childhood. It is a good place to slow down and see how much the shape of everyday life has changed.

Of course, a Beetle story belongs here. The small round car that gave this city its reason to exist became one of the best-selling cars ever made. It travelled far beyond Germany and became a symbol of very different things in different places — economy for some, freedom for others, and for many, simply family memories. When you see one in the exhibitions, take a moment. That modest shape is the reason this whole town, and this whole park, exists.

Now look back across the canal toward those four brick chimneys. They belong to the power station that was built to supply the factory. Their tall, plain forms have become a kind of city landmark, visible from far away. The factory buildings stretch along the water for a long distance, with long brick walls in a sober, functional style. Standing here, you see two faces of the same company at once: the polished visitor world on this side, and the working industrial world on the other.

Autostadt also thinks about the future, not only the past. Over the years, exhibitions here have explored questions about mobility, safety, energy, and how people will move through cities in the coming decades. Displays change from time to time, so what you find inside may differ from what a friend saw a few years ago. Children often enjoy the interactive areas, and in some places younger visitors can try simple driving experiences designed just for them.

There is a hotel on the site as well, and several restaurants and cafés where you can sit and rest. The food options range from quick snacks to more formal dining. If the weather is kind, the terraces near the water are pleasant places to pause. From there, you can watch boats move slowly along the canal, a quiet reminder that this waterway has carried goods across the region for a long time.

Before you move on, find a spot where you can see everything together. The glass towers catch the sky. The pavilions sit among the soft hills. The chimneys stand still across the water, and the factory hums faintly behind them. Somewhere a new owner is turning a key for the very first time. Take a breath, listen to the water once more, and notice how ordinary and how remarkable it is — a whole city built around the simple wish to move.

Practical tips

Allow most of a day to explore the pavilions and park.
Wear comfortable shoes for walking between scattered pavilions.
Visit the canal terraces for a quiet break with water views.
Check which temporary exhibitions are running before you arrive.
Watch a car handover near the towers for a lively scene.
