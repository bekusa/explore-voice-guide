The guide

The story

In the heart of the old town, a great cathedral of pale stone gathers around it the squares and lanes of medieval Valencia, its octagonal bell tower rising above the rooftops. This is the Cathedral of Valencia, a building of many centuries and styles, and, by one famous tradition, the home of the Holy Grail.

The cathedral was built over a long span beginning in the thirteenth century, on the site of earlier places of worship, so it mixes Gothic, Romanesque, and later Baroque elements, a record of the centuries that raised and changed it.

Its most famous claim is its greatest draw: a chapel within the cathedral holds an ancient stone cup that many believe to be the Holy Grail, the cup of the Last Supper. Whatever the truth, the relic has drawn pilgrims and the curious for centuries.

The bell tower, known locally as the Micalet, is an octagonal Gothic tower beside the cathedral, and climbing its winding stair to the top rewards you with a wide view over the rooftops, domes, and squares of the old town.

Inside, the cathedral is a calm, high space of Gothic arches, with chapels around the edges, fine paintings, and the relic chapel at its heart, a building to wander slowly rather than rush through.

The cathedral sits between two of the old town's loveliest squares, one before its main front and one beside it, both ringed with cafes and old buildings, so the building anchors a whole quarter of the historic centre.

One of those squares is the setting, on certain days, for an ancient water court, a tribunal that has met outdoors for centuries to settle disputes over the irrigation of the surrounding farmland, a living piece of medieval custom.

The mix of styles on the outside rewards a slow circuit: a Romanesque doorway here, a Gothic one there, a Baroque front on the main square, each from a different age of the cathedral's long life.

As the mother church of the city, it remains a working cathedral, with services and music, so visitors share it with worshippers and should treat it with the usual respect.

The surrounding old town, with its lanes, squares, and markets, spreads out from the cathedral, so it makes the natural centre of a day exploring the historic heart of Valencia on foot.

A few practical notes. Entry to the cathedral and the relic chapel is usually ticketed, the tower climb a little extra; dress modestly, and come in the morning to climb the tower before the heat. The squares around it are free to enjoy.

Stand in the relic chapel before the ancient cup, then climb the tower for the view over the old town, and the cathedral's blend of faith, legend, and long history comes together. When you step back out into the square, the cafes and the medieval water court carry the old city on into the present.
