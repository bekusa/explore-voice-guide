The guide

The story

Listen for the bell. A red trolley rolls along the rails of Buena Vista Street, and its bell rings over the sound of a brass band somewhere ahead. The pavement is warm under your feet. Shop windows painted with gold lettering catch the Southern California sun. For a moment, this does not feel like a theme park at all. It feels like a city street from about a century ago, built for one particular arrival.

That arrival is the story this street was designed to tell. In the 1920s, a young cartoonist named Walt Disney came west to Los Angeles with big ambitions and very little money. Buena Vista Street recreates the Los Angeles he would have found then — the streetcars, the Spanish-style facades, the optimism of a city still inventing itself. The name comes from Buena Vista Street in Burbank, where the Disney studio eventually settled.

Here is the first surprise. The park around you was not always this street. When Disney California Adventure opened in 2001, the entrance looked very different. The original design was playful and a little ironic, with giant postcard-style letters spelling out the state's name. Many visitors found it too thin. Over the following years, Disney carried out a major multi-year rebuild of the park, and Buena Vista Street, opened in 2012, was a centrepiece of that effort.

And here is a second surprise. The ground under this whole park was once a parking lot. For decades, cars filled this space in front of Disneyland, the original park that opened across the plaza in the 1950s. Today the two parks face each other across a shared entrance plaza. Disneyland looks to fantasy and the past. This park looks at California itself — its cities, its mountains, its coastline, and its movies.

Look toward the end of the street, and you will see a round tower with a tall spire. This is a recreation of the Carthay Circle Theatre, a real Los Angeles movie palace. The original theatre hosted the premiere of Snow White and the Seven Dwarfs in 1937. That film was Disney's first full-length animated feature, and its success changed the studio forever. The original building no longer stands, so this version keeps its memory alive. Inside, it serves as a restaurant.

Nearby, find the bronze statue of a young Walt and Mickey Mouse. It is called Storytellers. Unlike the more famous statue in Disneyland, which shows Walt as an established showman, this one shows him at the start — suitcase in hand, freshly arrived, with a small Mickey at his side. It is a quiet piece. Many people walk straight past it on their way to the rides. It is worth a pause.

From here, the park opens into themed districts, each one a different slice of California or of Disney storytelling. One of the most detailed is Cars Land, also opened in 2012. It recreates Radiator Springs, the desert town from the Pixar film Cars. The rock formations behind it, called the Cadillac Range, are shaped to suggest the tail fins of classic American cars. Look closely at the peaks, and you can pick out the curves of a vintage hood ornament.

The main ride there, Radiator Springs Racers, sends guests through the red-rock canyon and into a side-by-side race. At dusk, something gentle happens on the main street of the town. The neon signs on the motels and diners flicker on, one after another, often with music. It is a small moment, and it has become a ritual for many regular visitors. If you can, be standing on that street as the light fades.

Further along, the landscape shifts to the mountains. Grizzly Peak rises above a forested area designed to suggest California's national parks and wilderness. Its granite-like summit is shaped like the head of a bear, the animal on the state flag. Below it, Grizzly River Run sends round rafts down a churning river. Be warned — that ride is named honestly. Riders often leave very wet, so plan your bags and your camera accordingly.

In the same area, you will find Soarin', a flight simulation ride that lifts guests in front of a giant curved screen. The original version, called Soarin' Over California, flew riders above the state's own landscapes. Later versions took the flight around the world. Pay attention to your nose during the ride. The attraction releases scents to match the scenes, which surprises many first-time riders.

On the far side of the park, the waterfront tells another chapter. This area was once Paradise Pier, inspired by the old seaside boardwalks of the California coast. In 2018, much of it became Pixar Pier. The large wooden-style roller coaster here is now the Incredicoaster, themed to the Pixar film The Incredibles. The tall Ferris wheel, the Pixar Pal-A-Round, has some gondolas that slide and swing on tracks, and some that stay fixed. You can choose which kind to ride.

The park keeps changing. A tall tower that once held a Twilight Zone drop ride was rethemed in 2017 as Guardians of the Galaxy, Mission Breakout. Around it, Avengers Campus opened in 2021, with Marvel heroes walking among the crowds. Even the former Pacific Wharf area has been reimagined as San Fransokyo, from the film Big Hero 6. The layers of old and new sit side by side. Regulars can often point to a corner and tell you what used to stand there.

After dark, the lagoon at the heart of the waterfront becomes a stage. World of Color, a nighttime show, uses fountains, coloured light, and projections on sprays of water, set to Disney music. Crowds gather early along the railings. Even if you do not stay for the full show, watch the water in the quiet minutes before it starts. The surface is still, and the lights of the pier reflect across it.

As you walk back toward the entrance, notice how the park moves you through time and place — from a 1920s city street, to a desert highway town, to mountain forests, to an ocean boardwalk. Listen again for the trolley bell, and look up at the theatre tower against the sky. This place began as a parking lot. Now it holds a long story about California, and about one young man who came west with a suitcase and a sketchbook.

Good to know

Key facts

Disney California Adventure opened to the public in 2001.
The park was built on Disneyland's former parking lot.
Buena Vista Street and Cars Land both opened in 2012.
Original Carthay Circle Theatre hosted Snow White's 1937 premiere.
Paradise Pier was rethemed as Pixar Pier in 2018.
Look closer

What to look for

Car tail fin shapes in the Cadillac Range rock peaks.
The bear-head silhouette of Grizzly Peak's summit.
Storytellers statue of young Walt carrying a suitcase.
The red trolley running along Buena Vista Street.
Sliding gondolas versus fixed gondolas on the Pixar Pal-A-Round.
Before you go

Practical tips

Be on Radiator Springs' main street at dusk for the neon.
Pack a poncho or dry bag before riding Grizzly River Run.
Check the official Disneyland app for current ride wait times.
Claim a railing spot early for World of Color.
Visiting both parks in one day requires a park hopper ticket.
