The guide

The story

A short ride from the city, across a narrow strait, lies a small island given over almost entirely to leisure: beaches, theme parks, hotels, and attractions packed onto a green hump of land within sight of the harbour. This is Sentosa, Singapore's resort island and its purpose-built playground.

Sentosa is, in effect, one large resort, a whole island remade for fun. Where most city sights are about history or culture, this is about beaches, rides, shows, and family entertainment, a deliberate escape from the serious business of the mainland.

Part of the experience is the crossing. You can reach the island by a cable car gliding high over the harbour, by a short train, by road, or even on foot across a boardwalk, and the cable car ride is a small attraction in itself.

The island's beaches, made and groomed for visitors, line its southern shore, with calm water, palm trees, and bars. They are artificial and busy rather than wild, but they offer a rare chance to swim and laze within reach of the city.

Sentosa is best known for its large theme parks and attractions, including a major film-themed park, an aquarium, and a range of rides, shows, and family activities. These draw the biggest crowds and can fill an entire day on their own.

It is easy to forget that the island has a past. It was once a military fort guarding the harbour, and traces of that history survive among the modern attractions, a reminder of the strategic value this small island once held.

Despite the development, parts of the island keep greenery, walking trails, and viewpoints out over the sea and the busy shipping lanes, and a tall lookout and other vantage points give wide views back toward the city skyline.

The island is, above all, a family destination, designed to keep children busy from morning to night. For travellers with kids it can be the highlight of a trip; for others it may feel commercial and crowded, so it suits some visitors far more than others.

By night the island offers light-and-sound shows, beach bars, and dining, so it shifts from a daytime playground to an evening out, and many people stay into the night for the shows rather than returning to the city at dusk.

With so much packed onto the island, it pays to choose: a beach day, a theme-park day, or a mix, rather than trying to do everything. Most attractions are individually ticketed, so the costs add up quickly without a plan.

A few practical notes. Entry to the island is cheap, but the attractions are individually priced and can be expensive together; book the big parks ahead. Wear sun protection and light clothes, and allow a full day if you mean to do the major attractions.

Ride the cable car back toward the city at dusk, with the island's lights behind you and the towers ahead, and Sentosa makes sense as what it is: a small island that a city devoted entirely to play. Whether that delights or tires you, it is a pure expression of leisure. The serious city waits across the water.
