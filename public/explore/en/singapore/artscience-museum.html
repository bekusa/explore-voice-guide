The story

On the edge of the bay, beside the great three-towered resort, stands a building shaped like a giant white flower, or perhaps an open hand, its petals reaching up toward the sky. This is the ArtScience Museum, one of the most striking modern buildings in Singapore and a place where art, science, and technology meet.

The building is the work of the same architect behind the resort beside it, and its lotus-like form is deliberate: the curving petals collect rainwater that falls through the centre, and the whole shape was meant to evoke a welcoming open hand. It is a sculpture you can walk into.

As its name says, the museum sits at the meeting point of art and science, with exhibitions exploring how the two connect, from design and technology to the natural world. Its shows change often, so no two visits are quite the same.

Its most famous draw is a large, permanent immersive digital art experience, where rooms full of projected light, colour, and movement respond to visitors, with flowers blooming on the walls and water flowing across the floor. It has become one of the city's most photographed attractions.

Walking through the digital galleries, with images shifting around and beneath you, is less like viewing art than stepping inside it. Children and adults alike wander through entranced, and it blurs the line between exhibition, play, and spectacle.

Beyond the permanent digital galleries, the museum hosts major touring exhibitions on a wide range of subjects, from science and nature to film, design, and popular culture, so it is worth checking what is on before a visit.

The museum sits right on the waterfront of the bay, beside the resort and across from the financial district, in the heart of modern Singapore. Its white petals reflected in the surrounding pool are a favourite photograph, especially at dusk.

With its hands-on, immersive style, the museum is especially good for families and for anyone who finds traditional galleries dull. It is designed to be experienced and played with rather than studied in silence.

Because it sits among the bay's other attractions, the resort, the gardens, the promenade, and the evening light show, it slots easily into a half day around the waterfront, ideally ending with the show on the bay at night.

Even without going in, the building is worth seeing, a bold piece of modern architecture that has become part of the city's signature skyline alongside its neighbour. Many visitors photograph it from the promenade and the bridges nearby.

A few practical notes. Entry is ticketed, often by exhibition, and the popular digital galleries can sell out, so booking ahead is wise. It is air-conditioned, a cool stop in the heat, and easy to combine with the surrounding bayside sights.

Stand back on the promenade and take in the white petals against the towers and the water, then step inside into rooms of shifting light. The museum is a small argument that art and science are not opposites but partners. When you come back out to the bay, the modern city glitters around you.
