The guide

The story

The climb is short, but the air changes as you rise. Steps lead up from the street, and then the ground opens flat at the top. Calton Hill is one of several hills in Edinburgh, and one of the easiest to reach on foot. From here the whole city lies around you, and a strange set of monuments stands on the grass.

The first thing many people notice looks out of place. A row of tall stone columns stands alone, holding nothing up. This is the National Monument. It was begun in 1826 to honour Scottish soldiers and sailors who died in the wars against Napoleon. The plan was grand, modelled on an ancient temple in Athens.

Then the money ran out. Only twelve columns were ever raised before the work stopped for good. Some people mocked it and called it the city's folly. Others slowly grew fond of it. Today it stands unfinished, and that is its character. Climb up onto the base, and the columns frame the sky and the sea beyond.

Nearby rises a tall, dark tower shaped like an upturned telescope. This is the Nelson Monument, built to remember Admiral Nelson after the battle of Trafalgar. You can climb the narrow stair inside to a balcony near the top. The view rewards the effort, reaching out over the rooftops to the wide water beyond.

Look closely at the very top of the tower. A large ball sits on a mast there. Once a day it rises and then drops. This is the time ball. It falls at one o'clock, at the same moment the gun fires at the castle across the valley. Together they once gave ships two signals, one to see and one to hear.

A round building with columns stands a little apart from the rest. People often photograph it against the city behind. This is the Dugald Stewart Monument, raised for a professor of philosophy. Its simple circle of stone has become one of the shapes most closely linked with Edinburgh. At sunset the light gathers behind it.

Calton Hill also holds an old observatory. For many years astronomers worked here, measuring the stars and keeping precise time for the city. The domes and walls remain in place. The need for dark skies later pushed serious study away from the bright city lights, but the buildings still stand as a record of that work.

The hill has long been a place for people to gather. They come for the views, for festivals, and for the fires of an old winter celebration each year. The flat top makes room for crowds. On any ordinary evening you will find others here too, waiting quietly for the sky to change its colour.

Turn slowly, and the city explains itself. To the west runs the long ridge of the old town, ending at the castle on its rock. Below to the south sits the modern Scottish Parliament. To the north the streets fall away toward the Firth of Forth, the wide stretch of water that opens out to the sea.

The hill itself is old volcanic rock, like much of the high ground in Edinburgh. Long ago, ice and fire shaped this land together. The hard stone here resisted the grinding ice, and so it stayed high while softer ground wore down around it. That is why the views are so open in every direction today.

Because it sits within the city, Calton Hill is free to enter and open at all hours. People watch the sunrise here, and the sunset, and the lights coming on below. Few high places in any city are this easy to reach on foot. A few minutes of climbing buys a wide horizon and a quiet place to stand.

All this classical stone gave the city a nickname. People began to call Edinburgh the Athens of the North, partly because of the temple shapes gathered here on this hill. The comparison fit the city's long love of learning as well as its skyline. Standing here among the columns, with the old classical forms set against the open sky, it is easy to understand how the name first took hold and then lasted.

Stand among the columns as the light fades. The monuments around you were each meant to last forever, and each tells a slightly different story of ambition and memory. The wind moves steadily over the grass. Below, the city settles into evening. You take one more long look, then start back down toward the streets.
