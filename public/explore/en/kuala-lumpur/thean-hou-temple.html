The guide

The story

Climb the final steps and the color finds you. Red columns rise on every side. Hundreds of lanterns hang above, swaying a little in the warm air. Gold dragons curl along the roofs. Below, the city spreads out under the haze. This is Thean Hou Temple, set on a quiet hill in Kuala Lumpur.

The temple stands on a rise called Robson Heights. To reach the main hall you climb a wide stair. Stone lions guard the foot of the steps, worn smooth by many passing hands. As you go up, the noise of the streets fades behind you. At the top the air feels open. From the terrace you can see across the rooftops to the towers of the city beyond.

This temple was built by the Hainanese community, families whose roots lie on the island of Hainan in southern China. They raised the funds and shaped the design. The building took several years to complete. It opened officially in 1989. For the community it is both a place of prayer and a shared home.

The temple has six tiers, rising one above the other. This makes it one of the largest Chinese temples in Southeast Asia. Its many roofs curl up at the corners in the old style. Lifts and stairs both reach the upper floors, so the climb can be as gentle as you wish. Each level offers a new view and a new set of details to find. Take the climb slowly and look around.

The temple is named for the goddess Tian Hou, the Heavenly Mother. She is also called the goddess of the sea. Sailors and fishermen once prayed to her for safe passage. People far from the coast still seek her care. Her statue sits in the main hall, calm above the rows of candles.

She does not stand alone. Beside her you will find Guan Yin, known as the goddess of mercy. Nearby sits another guardian goddess of the waters. Three figures share the main altar. Worshippers light incense before them and bow. The smoke rises in thin lines and fills the hall with a soft, sweet scent.

Look up inside the hall. The ceiling and beams carry fine carving and bright paint. Reds, golds, and greens cover almost every surface. Pillars wrapped with carved dragons rise on either side of the main doors. Dragons and phoenixes wind across the wood. In old Chinese belief the dragon brings strength and the phoenix brings grace. Together they guard the space and those who pray.

Step back outside to the terrace. Rows of red lanterns hang above your head, tied in long lines. Some visitors write a wish on a ribbon and tie it where the lanterns hang. During the Chinese New Year their number grows, and at night they glow. At that time the temple fills with families, music, and light. It becomes one of the most crowded places in the city.

Along the railings you may find smaller shrines and signs. Some show the twelve animals of the Chinese zodiac. People search for their own birth animal and stop to read. Others spin a wheel or draw a fortune stick. These small acts are part of the visit for many of the guests here.

The temple is also a favorite place for weddings. Couples in fine clothes come to take photographs against the red columns and curved roofs. On a clear day the towers of the city stand sharp behind the temple's roofs. On a good day you may see more than one wedding party. The color of the building makes a bright background, and red itself is a sign of luck.

Dress with care, since this is a place of worship. Cover your shoulders and knees if you can. You may take photographs, but move gently around those who pray. Free incense is often offered near the hall. Climb to the upper levels for the widest view out across Kuala Lumpur and its towers.

Outside the main hall the grounds hold more to find. A small garden grows herbs once used in old remedies. A pond holds fish and the slow shapes of turtles. A tall statue of the goddess stands among the trees, looking out over the slope. People walk the paths between them, away from the crowd at the altar.

Before you go down the hill, stand at the rail and look out once more. Behind you the goddess of the sea keeps her quiet watch. In front of you the city hums on. Between the two, the red lanterns sway in the warm air. It is a calm place to pause, high above the streets.
