The guide

The story

Step through the gate and the sound finds you first. Birds call from the trees above, sharp and close. A large net rises overhead, so high you may forget it is there. Below it, birds fly freely between the branches. This is the Kuala Lumpur Bird Park, set inside the green of the Lake Gardens.

The park opened in 1991. It covers about 8.5 hectares of sloping ground, planted with tall trees and crossed by shaded paths. Streams run downhill through the grounds, and small bridges cross the water here and there. Much of it sits under one great canopy net. Inside that net the birds are not kept in small cages. They move, fly, and land where they please, close to your path.

For this reason the park calls itself the largest free-flight walk-in aviary in the world. You walk the same ground as the birds. One may step across the path ahead of you. Another may glide low over your head. There is little glass between you and them, only open air and a few low fences.

The park holds around 3,000 birds in all. They come from some 200 different kinds, gathered from Kuala Lumpur and far beyond. Some are local to the forests nearby. Others come from distant lands. Together they fill the air with many colors and many calls, from soft coos to loud and sudden cries.

Near the entrance you may meet the hornbills. These are large birds with great curved beaks. On top of the beak sits a strange shape called a casque, like a second crest. Hornbills matter in the culture of Borneo. Some here have been raised by the keepers, and they watch visitors with a steady, curious eye. Watch how they hop along the branches, heavy yet quick, and call to one another.

Walk on and you reach open lawns where peacocks roam. The males may spread their tails into a wide fan of green and blue. Each feather carries a round mark like an eye. When the fan opens fully, it is one of the great sights of the bird world. Step back and give them room.

Further in lies a pool where flamingos gather. They stand on thin legs, their feathers washed in pink. The color comes from the food they eat. Their legs seem to bend the wrong way, though what looks like a knee is really the ankle. They move slowly, bending long necks down to the water. In a group they look like a single soft cloud resting on the surface of the pool.

The park is shaped into zones as you climb the slope. Some areas let the birds fly free under the net. Others hold smaller enclosures for rare or delicate kinds. A waterfall feeds one of the pools, and its sound follows you along part of the path. Signs along the path name each bird and tell you where it comes from. Take your time to read them as you walk uphill.

There is a section for storks and other tall water birds. They wade in shallow pools and stand very still. Nearby, smaller songbirds dart between the leaves. Listen for the difference in their voices. The deep birds call low and slow, while the small ones answer quick and high above your head.

At set hours the park holds feeding sessions and short shows. Staff bring food and explain the habits of each bird. Some birds have learned simple tasks, and may sort colors or fly low over the seated crowd. Children gather close to watch them. Check the daily times when you arrive, so you can plan your walk around the moments you most want to see.

Wear shoes you can walk in, since the paths rise and fall across the hill. Bring water and perhaps a hat. The tree cover gives shade, but the air stays warm and damp. Move slowly and look up often. The best moments here come when you simply stop and watch one bird closely.

In one area you can stand among smaller parrots and lories. Bright birds in red, green, and blue may land on a visitor's arm to feed. Keepers hand out small cups of nectar. The birds drink quickly, then lift away again. It is a chance to see their colors up close, and to feel how light they are.

Before the exit, pause on a quiet path and listen once more. So many wings, so many voices, all under one wide net in the middle of a capital city. For a short while you share their green world. Then you step back out through the gate, and the city sound returns around you.
