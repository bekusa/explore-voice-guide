The guide

The story

The smell of incense and wood smoke meets you first. A bell rings out across the water. Below you, a wide gray river moves slowly past stone steps. On the far bank stands a temple with a golden roof. This is Pashupatinath, one of the holiest places of the Hindu faith. People come here from across Nepal and India.

The temple stands on the banks of the Bagmati River, in the east of Kathmandu. It is dedicated to Shiva, here called Pashupati, the lord of all living beings. For many centuries, pilgrims have traveled long distances to pray at this spot. It is among the most sacred Shiva temples anywhere.

Look across the river at the main temple. It has a two-level roof covered in gold. Its doors are plated in silver. The style is called pagoda, with each roof smaller than the one below. Only Hindus may enter the inner courtyard. From the eastern bank, where you stand, everyone can see it and take it in.

The temple you see today was raised in its present form in the seventeenth century. But people have worshiped on this site for far longer. Older shrines once stood here and were rebuilt many times. The exact beginning is lost to time. What remains is an unbroken thread of devotion, carried across the generations.

This is also a place of death, and of farewell. Along the river are stone platforms called ghats. Here, Hindu families bring their dead for cremation. The body is washed in the river, then placed on a fire of wood. The ashes are given to the water. You may see this happen during your visit.

Watch with respect and from a distance. For the families, this is a sacred and tender moment. Do not point. Lower your camera near the fires. In this faith, death is not hidden away. It happens in the open, beside the river, as part of the flow of life. Standing here invites a quiet, honest pause.

The river itself is called the Bagmati. It is holy to both Hindus and Buddhists. Its water flows south, and in time reaches the sacred Ganges. For pilgrims, to be cremated on its banks is a great blessing. The river carries prayers, ashes, and the marigold flowers left as offerings. Its current runs cold and swift after the monsoon rains.

Look for the holy men known as sadhus. They wear orange robes, or smear their skin with gray ash. Some have long, matted hair. They have given up ordinary life to follow Shiva. Many of them sit near the temple gates. If you wish to photograph one, ask first. Some will expect a small gift in return.

Near the temple stands a large statue of a bull. It is Nandi, the faithful mount of Shiva. Its body is cast in metal and shines from many hands. Bells hang at the shrine doors. Pilgrims ring them as they enter. Around the wider grounds are rows of small white shrines. Each one shelters a stone lingam. This simple upright form stands for Shiva. Pilgrims pour water and milk over them. They lay down flowers and red powder. Incense smoke hangs in the air. These small acts repeat all day long.

Behind the temple, a forest climbs the hillside. Deer live among the trees, in a place tied to an old story about Shiva. Monkeys move along the walls and rooftops. Keep your bags closed and your food out of sight. The animals are bold and quick. They know the habits of visitors well.

Each year, a great festival fills these grounds. It is called Maha Shivaratri, the night of Shiva. Hundreds of thousands of pilgrims arrive. Holy men come from across the region. Lamps and fires burn through the night. If you visit on an ordinary day, the grounds are calmer, and you can move at your own pace.

Cross one of the bridges and climb the steps on the far side. From the terraces above, you can look back over the whole scene. The golden roof shines in the light. Smoke rises from the ghats below. The river bends away between the banks. From here, the size and meaning of the place become clear.

Find a quiet step and sit for a while. Watch the smoke drift and the water move. Listen to the bells and the soft sound of prayer. This is a place where life and death stand close together, in plain sight. Carry that stillness with you when you rise to leave.
