Chiang Mai Night Bazaar

Come as the sun goes down.

⏱ 5 min
▶BeginOpen the Lokali app

The story

Come as the sun goes down. The heat of the day begins to lift. One by one, the lights switch on along the street, strings of bulbs and glowing paper lanterns. Stalls unfold and fill with goods. The smell of grilling meat and sweet fruit drifts through the warm air. The evening market is waking up, and the street belongs to walkers now.

You are on Chang Klan Road. It runs north to south, a short walk east of the old city moat. Each evening this stretch of pavement turns into one of the largest night markets in the north of Thailand. Stalls line both sides. Behind them, covered halls and older buildings hold still more sellers, row upon row.

The market opens every single night, in the dry season and the wet. This sets it apart from the city's walking streets, which appear only on certain evenings of the week. Here, there is always something to see, whatever day you happen to arrive. Locals shop for everyday things alongside travelers hunting for gifts, and the crowd grows thick and slow as the night deepens.

This corner of the city has long been tied to trade. In older times, caravans crossed these lands carrying goods between southern China and the towns of the north. Traders known as the Haw, many of them from the region of Yunnan, traveled the routes with their mules. The story goes that this area grew as a place of exchange, and the market that stands here now carries on that long habit of buying and selling.

Look first at the textiles. Many come from the hill peoples who live in the mountains around the city. You will see cloth dyed in deep indigo, embroidered panels, woven bags, and scarves in bright thread. Beside them sit trays of silver, worked into bracelets, rings, and earrings. Each piece carries the marks of the hand that shaped it. Take a moment to feel the weight and the weave before you decide.

There is far more to find. Carvers sell figures and bowls cut from teak and other woods. Lacquerware shines black and gold under the lamps. Painters offer canvases of temples, rice fields, and elephants. Paper lanterns sway overhead. Soaps, spices, t-shirts, bags, and small souvenirs cover the tables in a long, changing river of goods.

Follow your nose to the food. Open kitchens send up smoke and steam. You can find northern Thai dishes, grilled skewers, noodle bowls, and sticky rice. Stalls pile fresh fruit in slices, cool and ready to eat. Cold drinks sweat in tubs of ice. Seating areas let you sit, rest your feet, and eat among the crowd.

The market has its own rhythm. Sellers call out gently to passers-by. Buyers stop, lift an item, weigh it in the hand, and ask the price. Some bargain with a smile and a slow back and forth; others simply nod and move on. Music plays from somewhere down the street. The sound of many voices blends into a steady hum that rises and falls as you walk along the crowded pavement.

It helps to know the difference between the markets. The Night Bazaar runs here on Chang Klan Road every night of the week. The famous walking streets are separate. One fills Ratchadamnoen Road inside the old city on Sundays. Another fills Wualai Road on Saturdays. If your nights are few, this market is the one always open.

Give yourself room to wander without a plan. Step off the main pavement into the covered halls and the older market buildings. The deeper rows often hold quieter sellers and different goods. It is easy to lose your bearings here, so note a landmark, and do not worry if you double back across the same lane twice.

A few simple habits help. Ask the price with a friendly tone, and offer a little less in return. Keep your money in a front pocket and your bag closed in the crowd. Carry small notes for easy paying. And take your time, because the pleasure here is as much in the looking as in the buying.

Later, when your hands are full and your feet are tired, step to the edge of the street. Watch the lights and the slow flow of people for a moment. Markets like this have stood in this city for a very long time, in one form or another, under lamplight and starlight. You have just joined a habit of trade that is older than memory itself.

Key facts

Located on Chang Klan Road, east of the moat.
Open nightly throughout the year, in any season.
Among the largest night markets in northern Thailand.
Area linked to historic Yunnanese Haw caravan trade.
Separate from the Saturday and Sunday walking streets.

What to look for

Strings of bulbs and glowing paper lanterns overhead.
Open kitchens grilling skewers and steaming noodles.
Indigo-dyed cloth and embroidered hill-tribe panels.
Trays of handmade silver bracelets and rings.

Practical tips

Bargain politely and offer a little below the asking price.
Keep cash in a front pocket within the crowd.
Arrive after sunset when stalls and lights open.
Explore the covered halls behind the street stalls.
