The guide

The story

A doorman in a tall turban opens the door. Cool air flows out to meet you, carrying the faint scent of flowers. Inside, a wide staircase curves upward beneath a high dome. Marble catches the light. This is one of the grand hotels of the city, and it rises right beside the sea. Its name is known to travelers far beyond Mumbai.

This is the Taj Mahal Palace Hotel. It stands in Colaba, on the southern shore of Mumbai, next to the Gateway of Mumbai. The two landmarks face the harbor together. Ships once passed close by. Guests have arrived here for more than a hundred years, by carriage, by car, and by boat from the water. The hotel has seen the city grow up around it.

The hotel opened in 1903. It was the idea of Jamsetji Tata, a businessman who founded one of Mumbai's largest companies. He wanted to give the city a hotel of the highest standard. The building cost a great sum for its day. When it opened, it offered comforts that few people in the city had ever seen. For its guests, a night here was something to remember.

A famous story explains why Tata built it. People say he was once refused entry to a European hotel because he was Mumbain, and built his own grand hotel in answer. Historians are not sure this is true. It may be only legend. But the story has lasted, because it speaks to the pride of a city under foreign rule.

The design mixes many styles. You can see Moorish arches, European touches, and details drawn from across the East. The best known feature is the red dome that crowns the building. It can be seen from the harbor and from the streets nearby. For many travelers arriving by sea, it was the first landmark they knew. The dome told them they had reached Bombay at last.

The hotel was modern for its time. It brought comforts that were new to the city, from electric lights to elevators. The staff were trained to a high standard. Over the years, kings and presidents, writers and film stars have stayed within these walls. The guest book reads almost like a record of the past century itself. Each famous name adds another layer to the story.

Look at the building from the street. The grand entrance faces the city, not the sea. A story says the architect meant it to face the water, and despaired when he saw his mistake. This too is most likely a myth. The design was probably planned this way from the start, to greet guests who arrived by land.

Walk around the outside and notice the detail. Balconies, arches, and carved stone repeat across the long facade. The building is large, filling a full block of the street. It was made to last, and to impress, and it has done both. More than a hundred years on, it still draws every eye. The taller tower beside it was added much later, during the 1970s.

The hotel has known hard times as well. In November 2008, it was one of several places in the city attacked by gunmen. Many people lost their lives across the city during those days. The hotel itself was damaged and later carefully restored. Today a quiet memorial inside honors those who died here. The hotel chose to remember rather than to forget.

After the repairs, the hotel reopened its doors. The restoration kept the old details intact. Skilled workers matched the original stone and plaster. The staircase, the domes, and the halls look much as they did a century ago. The building now carries both its old glamour and the memory of that harder time. It stands today as proud as ever.

You do not have to be a guest to enjoy it. Many visitors come simply to stand outside, between the hotel and the Gateway, and take in the view. The two landmarks frame the entrance to old Bombay. Together they tell the story of the city's long meeting with the wider world, by trade and by sea. Few places hold that past so plainly.

Pause here as the harbor light changes. The red dome holds the last of the sun. Behind you, the sea moves quietly against the wall. This building has welcomed travelers through a century of change, through pride and sorrow and renewal. Stand still for a moment, and let that long story settle around you before you walk on.
