The guide

The story

Listen for a moment. Under the music drifting from hidden speakers, you can hear the soft rumble of a roller coaster launching somewhere ahead. There is a scream from a tall pale building on the far right. Underfoot, the pavement is warm, often already hot by mid-morning. Above you, the Florida sky is huge and very blue. You are standing at the start of a street that was built to look like another city, on the other side of the country, in another decade.

This is Disney's Hollywood Studios. It sits within Walt Disney World, in Orange County, Florida, a short drive from the city of Orlando. The park opened in 1989 under a different name: Disney-MGM Studios. That name came from a licensing deal with the old Hollywood studio. In 2008, the park took the name you see today. The idea from the start was simple and bold. This would be a theme park about the movies themselves, about how films are made and the dream of Hollywood.

Take a look at the entrance gate behind you, or the one you just walked through. Notice its curved lines, its rounded fins, its stripes of white and colour. This style is called Streamline Moderne, a late branch of Art Deco that loved smooth curves and the look of speed. The gate takes inspiration from the Pan-Pacific Auditorium, a famous exhibition hall that once stood in Los Angeles. The real building is gone. Here, a version of it still greets thousands of people every day.

Now walk forward onto Hollywood Boulevard. The shop fronts are not copies of single buildings. They are a blend, a kind of idealised Los Angeles of the 1930s and 1940s. Look up at the neon signs, the painted lettering, the chrome trim. Just inside the entrance, a small tower with a spire rises from the plaza, and a figure of Mickey Mouse stands on top. It echoes Crossroads of the World, a real landmark on Sunset Boulevard in Los Angeles. Most visitors walk straight past it.

Here is the first surprise. In its early years, this park was not only a stage set. It was a working production facility. There were sound stages, a backlot tour, and a real animation studio. Disney artists in Florida drew large parts of animated features in the 1990s and early 2000s, including much of Mulan and Lilo and Stitch. Guests could watch animators at their desks through glass windows. The animation studio closed in the early 2000s, and the park slowly shifted from showing filmmaking to putting you inside the stories.

At the far end of the boulevard stands the red pagoda-style theatre. It is modelled on the famous Chinese Theatre in Hollywood, where film stars press their hands into wet cement. For nearly three decades, this building held The Great Movie Ride, a long tour through classic film scenes. Today it houses Mickey and Minnie's Runaway Railway, which opened in 2020. The ride takes you into a cartoon world, where the flat screen seems to fold open around you. Check the forecourt for handprints in the pavement.

Turn right now and walk onto Sunset Boulevard. The street curves gently uphill, and at the top a tall, cracked, weathered hotel rises against the sky. This is the Twilight Zone Tower of Terror, which opened in 1994. The story is that the Hollywood Tower Hotel was struck by lightning on a stormy night in 1939, and several guests vanished from an elevator. The building has never been repaired. Look at the dead ivy, the faded signs, and the scorched edges on the facade.

Here is the second surprise. The designers chose the hotel's colours and style so that, from many places in the park, it blends with the sky. They also wanted it to fit with the Moroccan-style buildings of a neighbouring park, Epcot, when seen from certain angles. It is a trick of planning. A giant building can feel like part of the horizon instead of a wall. Inside, the drops are random. You will not know when the floor falls away.

Across from the tower, the Rock 'n' Roller Coaster sits under a giant guitar. It launches riders from a standstill in a few seconds, in the dark, with music loud in the speakers by each seat. Further along, a large outdoor amphitheatre faces a lagoon. This is the home of Fantasmic, the nighttime show of water, fire, light and projections on curtains of spray. People come early to find a seat, and the low hum of a waiting crowd fills the evening air.

Now the park changes scale. Head toward Toy Story Land, which opened in 2018. The idea here is that you have shrunk to the size of a toy and wandered into a child's backyard. Giant building blocks, oversized crayons, and huge footprints in the concrete surround you. The lampposts look like plastic toys. Even the fence is made of enormous lolly sticks. Children love it, but adults often stop to stare at the craftsmanship of the giant board games and the scale of everyday objects.

Beyond it, the scenery turns to rock spires and weathered stone. This is Star Wars: Galaxy's Edge, which opened in 2019. It is set in Black Spire Outpost, a trading village on a distant planet called Batuu. A full-size Millennium Falcon sits in its docking bay, scratched and patched. Staff here often greet you with the phrase bright suns, the local hello. Rise of the Resistance, the land's largest attraction, mixes walking scenes, vehicles, and huge sets into one long story.

Between the big lands, look for quieter corners. Near Echo Lake, a green dinosaur stands at the water's edge. Her name is Gertie, a tribute to one of the earliest animated characters, from a short cartoon made more than a century ago. She serves ice cream. Nearby, a replica of the Hollywood Brown Derby restaurant keeps the old studio-era style alive, with framed caricatures of stars on its walls. These small places carry the park's original spirit: a love letter to the history of film.

Before you move on, pause where you are. Listen again to the layers of music, each street with its own soundtrack chosen for its era or its world. Watch how the light hits the white curves of the boulevard, and how the tower rises in the distance. Everything here was built to help you feel inside a story. As you walk on, notice the small things, a painted sign or a worn step, that someone placed with care, just for you to find.

Before you go

Practical tips

Head to Rise of the Resistance early in the day.
Arrive at Fantasmic well ahead to find good seats.
Carry a refillable water bottle for the Florida heat.
Use the Disney Skyliner gondola to reach Epcot area resorts.
Check the Disney app for return times on popular rides.
