The guide

The story

Step in under the trees, and the air changes. It is cooler here, and much quieter. The noise of the town fades away behind you. Tall trunks rise on every side. The ground is soft with fallen leaves, and it gives a little underfoot. You are walking onto a narrow neck of land that reaches out into Lake Wakatipu.

Queenstown Gardens sits on a small peninsula. Water lies on three sides of you. The land was set aside as a public reserve in the nineteenth century. This was not long after gold first drew settlers to the valley. People wanted a calm, green place to walk and rest, away from the noise and hard work of the growing town.

Many of the trees here came from far away. Early gardeners planted oaks from Europe and tall conifers from North America. There are giant sequoias, among the largest tree species on earth. Stand beneath one and look up, and the top seems very far above you. In autumn, the oaks and maples turn gold, orange, and deep red before the leaves fall.

Follow the path around the edge of the peninsula. It runs close to the water for much of the way. Through gaps in the trees you catch the lake, bright and cold, and the mountains beyond it. The full loop is gentle and slow, and takes about half an hour. Cyclists and runners share the wider paths, so keep to one side as you go.

Near the centre of the gardens is a rose garden. In summer the beds are full of colour and scent. Narrow stone paths cross between them. Wooden benches sit in the shade nearby. They are placed for people who simply want to stop, rest, breathe the warm air, and look slowly around at the flowers.

You may notice people throwing flat discs between the trees. This is a frisbee golf course, one of the older ones in New Zealand. Players aim for metal baskets set along a winding route through the park. The discs hum softly as they fly. Step aside, let them pass, and watch how each one curves and settles through the air.

Water birds gather at the quieter edges of the gardens. Ducks rest on the grass and slip into the shallows. In the trees overhead, smaller birds call and move between the branches. Their songs mix with the wind in the leaves. Behind it all runs the distant, steady sound of the lake meeting the shore.

There is a war memorial within the gardens. It honours local people who died in the wars of the twentieth century. The mood around it is quiet and still. Visitors often pause here for a moment. They read the names carved into the stone, stand a while in silence, and then walk slowly on.

The gardens hold older pastimes too. There are lawns kept smooth for the game of bowls, where players roll weighted balls across the grass toward a target. There are courts for tennis nearby. On a fine afternoon you may hear the soft click of the bowls touching, and low voices keeping the score. These games have been played here for generations.

Look closely at the trunks of the oldest trees. Some are wide enough that two people could not reach around them. Their bark is deeply lined and rough under the hand. These trees were small saplings when the town was young. They have stood through every season since, growing slowly and steadily, year upon year upon year.

Down at the shore, the land meets the lake in rounded grey stones. The water is clear and very cold to the touch. Small waves move the pebbles with a soft rattle. Smooth driftwood collects in the little bays along the edge. From here the view opens wide across the lake to the long line of peaks on the far side.

In every season the gardens feel different. Spring brings blossom and bright new leaves. Summer brings deep shade and the scent of roses. Autumn turns the whole place gold and red. In winter the bare branches trace dark lines against the snow on the distant mountains. Each visit shows the same quiet paths in a different light.

Find a bench near the water and sit for a while. Let the town wait a little longer. The trees move gently in the wind above you. The lake shines beyond them, wide and bright. In the middle of a busy place, the gardens hold a small, steady pocket of calm, kept here for anyone who stops to find it.
