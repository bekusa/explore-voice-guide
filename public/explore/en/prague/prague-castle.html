The guide

The story

Climb the hill above the river, and the castle appears all at once. It is not one single building. It is a small city of palaces, churches, and courtyards. Towers rise behind high stone walls. Spires reach up into the sky. Guards stand still at the gates. This is Prague Castle. It has watched over the city for more than a thousand years. Pause a moment before you go in.

Many people expect a single fortress. Instead you find a long ridge covered in buildings. There are grand palaces and quiet gardens. There are chapels, halls, and narrow lanes. The record books call it the largest ancient castle complex in the world. It covers almost seventy thousand square meters. You could spend a whole day here and still not see it all. Wear comfortable shoes for the uneven stone paths.

The story begins around the year 880. A prince named Borivoj moved his seat to this hill. He came from the Premyslid family, the first rulers of the land. The choice was wise. The hill rises high above the Vltava River. From here a ruler could watch the valley and defend it. A small settlement already stood close by.

Over the centuries the castle grew and changed. Each ruler added something of his own. You can read the building styles like layers in rock. There are Romanesque stones from the tenth century. There are Gothic arches from the fourteenth century. Later came Renaissance halls and Baroque fronts. Somehow the castle holds all these ages together in one place.

For most of its long life this was a seat of power. Bohemian princes and kings lived here. Holy Roman emperors held their court here. Since 1918 it has served as the home of the country's president. Look up at the main palace. When the flag is flying above it, the president is inside.

Enter through the courtyards. Each one opens into the next. Guards in uniform stand watch at the gates. On the hour they change places in a slow and careful routine. A larger ceremony takes place at midday. Many visitors gather to watch it. Find a good spot a little early if you want to see it well. The guards wear lighter uniforms in summer and darker ones in the colder months.

At the heart of the castle stands the great cathedral. Its dark towers rise high above the walls around it. You will probably notice it before anything else. Inside are royal tombs, side chapels, and colored windows. We will explore it more fully in its own guide. For now, step back and take in its sheer height.

Close by stands the Old Royal Palace. Step inside its great hall. The ceiling spreads above you in curving stone ribs. Long ago, knights rode horses up a wide staircase into this very room. Contests of skill were held indoors, out of the weather. The hall has also hosted coronations and great meetings of state.

Do not miss the small street called Golden Lane. Its little houses are brightly painted in soft colors. They lean against the old castle wall. Craftsmen, archers, and guards once lived in them. In later years, writers took rooms here too. As you walk through, you must lower your head in some of the low doorways.

The castle has known many hard times. Fires have damaged it. It has stood empty and neglected for long stretches. It has seen wars and sudden changes of power. Some of the most important events in the country's history began behind these walls. Each time trouble came, the castle survived and was renewed.

Make your way to the southern gardens or the eastern viewpoint. From there the whole city lies open below you. Red rooftops stretch toward the horizon. The river curves through the center. The bridges cross it one after another. From up here, you finally understand why a ruler would choose this hill. Try to arrive near sunset, when warm light spreads across the rooftops.

Take your time inside the walls. There are quiet corners away from the crowds. There are small gardens, old wells, and shaded benches. Sit for a while and watch the light move across the stone. The castle rewards those who slow down and simply look.

Prague Castle is not a single moment in time. It is a thousand years stacked up in stone. Princes, emperors, and presidents have all called it home. As you walk back out through the gate, turn and look once more. The castle will still be standing here long after your visit has ended. Carry the quiet of this place with you.

Key facts

Founded around the year 880 by Prince Borivoj.
Largest ancient castle complex, almost seventy thousand square meters.
Seat of the country's president since 1918.

What to look for

Stone ribs spreading across the Old Royal Palace ceiling.
Tiny painted houses along narrow Golden Lane.
Spires of the great cathedral above the walls.
A flag flying when the president is in residence.

Practical tips

Buy tickets online to skip the longest entry lines.

Allow at least three hours to see everything.
Visit the southern gardens for views across Prague.
