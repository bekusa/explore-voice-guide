The guide

The story

Listen first. Somewhere beyond the trees, a chain lift clacks in a steady rhythm. Then comes a pause, a rush of wind, and a wave of screams that rises and fades like weather moving across a field. Underfoot the asphalt is warm, the air carries fried dough and sunscreen, and above the treeline a familiar silhouette rises in iron lattice. You are in rural Virginia, not Paris. Welcome to Kings Dominion.

The park sits in Doswell, in the northern part of Hanover County, about a half-hour drive north of Richmond. Interstate 95 runs close by, and that location matters. The highway connects Washington, Richmond and the Carolinas, and the park was placed where millions of travellers already passed. Before the roller coasters came, this was quiet farm and forest country. Much of the land around the park still looks that way.

Kings Dominion opened in 1975. It was developed by the same company behind Kings Island, a park near Cincinnati in Ohio, and the two shared a family resemblance from the start. The name has a double meaning. Virginia has long been called the Old Dominion, a nickname from the colonial era, when the territory was a dominion of the English crown. Pair that with the Kings brand, and the park had a title that sounded both local and grand.

Now look up at the tower. It is a scaled-down replica of the Eiffel Tower, roughly one third the height of the Paris original. Kings Island in Ohio has a similar one, and the two towers are a shared signature of those early parks. An elevator carries visitors to an observation deck near the top. From there the park spreads out like a map, and the Virginia countryside rolls away in every direction... a green sea of trees broken by ribbons of steel track.

Here is a detail many visitors miss. The tower is not just a photo backdrop. It works as the park's compass. Main walkways radiate around it, and the fountains in front of it form the central plaza. If you ever lose your bearings, find the tower. Almost every path eventually brings you back to its base.

For many families in Virginia, the park's real heart is a wooden coaster that opened with the park itself. For decades it was known as the Rebel Yell, a racing coaster with two side-by-side tracks, so two trains run against each other. In 2018 the park renamed it Racer 75, a nod to its opening year. Wooden coasters feel different from steel ones. You hear the timber creak, you feel the train shudder and rattle, and the ride seems almost alive.

If the Racer is the park's history, Intimidator 305 is its statement. It opened in 2010, and the number in its name refers to the height of its first drop: 305 feet. The coaster carries a NASCAR theme, honouring the racing driver Dale Earnhardt, whose nickname was The Intimidator. Watch the first plunge from the ground. The track drops steeply, and the train races into tight, low turns across the hillside. Riders often describe a strong pull on the body in those turns.

Between these two extremes, the park layers in a wide range of rides. There are wooden coasters and steel coasters, spinning rides and water rides, and gentler attractions for young children. One of the more striking newer coasters is Twisted Timbers, which opened in 2018. It was rebuilt from an older wooden ride, using a hybrid design: a wooden support structure with steel track on top. The result keeps the look of a classic woodie but adds inversions that the original could never perform.

And here is the second surprise. Look closely at that hybrid coaster's frame. The tall grid of timber is a direct link to the earlier ride that stood in its place. Instead of tearing everything down, the builders reused much of the old wooden structure. Amusement parks do this more often than people realise. Rides are retired, renamed, repainted and reborn, so a walk through the park is also a walk through layers of earlier attractions.

Ownership has changed hands over the decades too. The park passed through several companies after its founding. In 2006 it became part of Cedar Fair, the operator of Cedar Point in Ohio. In 2024 Cedar Fair merged with Six Flags, and Kings Dominion now sits within that larger company. For visitors, these changes show up in small ways: new ride names, new themes, new seasonal events, while the tower and the old wooden coasters remain.

The park also changes with the calendar. Summer brings long days, heat rising off the pavement and lines that stretch in the afternoon sun. A separate water park area on the grounds offers slides and pools during the warm months. Then autumn arrives. The park has a long tradition of Halloween events, and in the evenings the paths fill with fog machines, costumed performers and haunted attractions. The same coasters feel very different in the dark.

Step back for a moment and think about where you are. Hanover County has deep roots in American history. Patrick Henry, the revolutionary orator, was born in this county, and old taverns and courthouses still stand in its small towns. A short drive takes you from colonial brick buildings to a 305-foot steel coaster. That contrast is part of the character of this place: old Virginia and modern thrill-seeking, sharing the same green landscape.

As you walk, notice how the park sounds in layers. There is the clatter of wooden track, the deeper roar of steel wheels, the splash of water rides, music from the speakers, and children laughing near the smaller rides. Notice the smells too. Popcorn, funnel cake, warm grease from the fryers and, after a summer storm, wet pavement steaming in the sun. These details are the same ones that generations of Virginia families remember from their first visit.

Before you move on, look once more toward the tower. Let your eyes follow the lattice from the fountains to the top. Around you, people are queuing, eating, resting on benches, comparing which ride scared them most. Somewhere a chain lift begins to clack again. Take a breath, choose your next path, and remember that every road in this park leads back to that small iron tower in the middle of the Virginia woods.

Before you go

Practical tips

Ride the biggest coasters first, before afternoon lines build.
Use the tower as your landmark if you get lost.
Bring sun protection for long, exposed summer queues.
Check the park calendar for autumn evening Halloween events.
Pack a change of clothes if visiting the water park.
