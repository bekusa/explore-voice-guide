The guide

The story

A meadow opens in front of you, green and soft and wide. Pine trees stand dark at its edges. The grass is cool under your feet. You lift your eyes, and a giant wall of ice and rock fills the sky. This is Fairy Meadows, and the mountain above it is Nanga Parbat.

The meadow lies at about three thousand three hundred meters above the sea. The air is clean and a little thin. In summer, wildflowers grow among the grass in yellow, white, and purple. Wooden huts stand here and there across the slope. There are no paved roads inside the meadow. There are no large hotels. The place stays simple and small, close to the way it has always been.

Above the meadow rises Nanga Parbat. It reaches eight thousand one hundred and twenty-six meters into the sky. It is the ninth highest mountain on the whole planet. From Fairy Meadows, you look straight at its northern side. This face is called the Raikot face. It is a vast slope of snow, ice, and bare gray stone.

Climbers have long given this mountain a hard name. They call it the killer mountain. Many people have died trying to reach its top. The first climbers to stand on the summit did so in 1953. The mountain looks calm from the meadow, but it demands deep respect from anyone who climbs it.

The name Fairy Meadows did not come from this region. German climbers gave it the name many years ago. In their language, they called it the meadow of fairy tales. They camped here on their way to the mountain. The soft green field, set below the great peak, must have looked like a scene from an old story.

Getting here is part of the experience, and it is not easy. From Gilgit, you travel about eighty kilometers along the Karakoram Highway. This famous road follows the Indus River through deep, dry gorges. After a couple of hours, you reach a place called Raikot Bridge. There the wide paved road ends, and a very different road begins.

At Raikot Bridge, you change to a jeep. The track that climbs from here is narrow and steep. It clings to the side of the mountain. On one side rises rock. On the other side falls a long drop. Local drivers know every turn of this road. The ride is slow, rough, and not for the faint of heart.

The jeep cannot take you all the way. It stops at a small village called Tato. From there, you walk. The trail climbs through forest for a few kilometers. You may feel the thin air slow your steps. Take your time. Then the trees open, and the meadow spreads out before you at last.

When you arrive, the first thing to do is simply look. The white face of Nanga Parbat stands across the valley. In clear weather, the peak is sharp against the blue. Clouds often gather around it in the afternoon. Light moves over the ice through the day, and the mountain never looks the same for long.

Below the meadow lies the Raikot Glacier. It is a long river of ice that flows down from the mountain. You can walk toward its edge along marked paths. The ice groans and cracks as it moves, far too slowly for the eye to see. Cold air drifts off its surface. Where the ice melts, streams of pale gray water run down toward the valley.

Life in the meadow is quiet and slow. At night, there is no city light to dim the sky. The stars come out in great number, thick from edge to edge. The cold settles in fast after the sun drops behind the ridges. People gather close to fires and warm food. Sound carries far in the still mountain air, and then the silence returns.

This is a place to move gently and stay a while. Walk the meadow in the early morning, when the light is soft and low. Sit at the tree line and watch the mountain change. Listen to the wind in the pines and the water in the streams. Drink tea on a wooden porch and let the hours pass. There is little to do here, and that is the point.

Nanga Parbat has stood above this meadow for longer than anyone can hold in mind. People come, rest in the grass, and go home again. The mountain stays. When you leave Fairy Meadows, you carry the shape of that great white wall with you, quiet in your memory.
