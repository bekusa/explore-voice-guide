The guide

The story

Behind the coral-stone walls of an old fort near the creek, in the oldest building in the city, lies a museum that tells how Dubai changed from a small desert and pearling town into a city of skyscrapers within living memory. This is the Dubai Museum, the best place to grasp how recent and how rapid that change has been.

The fort itself is the first exhibit. Built in the late eighteenth century to guard the town and its creek, it is the oldest surviving building in Dubai, with thick walls, towers, and a courtyard, a rare survival from the era before oil and glass towers.

Inside and below, the museum sets out the old way of life: fishing, pearl diving, trade by sea, life in the desert, and the close-knit town that clustered along the creek. Models, recreated scenes, and old photographs bring that vanished world close.

The story it tells is astonishing for its speed. Within the lifetime of older residents, the city went from a modest creekside settlement to a global metropolis, and the museum lays the two side by side, the old town and the new towers, in a way that no skyline alone can.

The pearling trade, once the town's lifeblood, gets particular attention, with displays on the divers who risked their lives gathering oysters from the seabed before the trade collapsed and oil transformed everything.

Recreated scenes of old streets, shops, and homes let you walk through a version of the town as it was, with figures and sound, a simple but effective way to feel the daily life of the past.

The contrast with the city outside is the whole point. Step out of the fort and the modern metropolis surrounds you; step in, and you are in the world it replaced, all within a few paces.

The museum sits in the old heart of the city, beside the historic district and the creek, so it pairs naturally with a walk through the restored old quarter and a boat across the water.

It is worth checking the museum's current status before visiting, as it has been subject to renovation, and arrangements can change; the fort and the surrounding historic district reward a visit regardless.

Compared with the city's giant modern attractions, the museum is small, modest, and inexpensive, a quiet counterpoint to the towers and malls, and all the more valuable for it.

A few practical notes. Entry is cheap; the old quarter around it is best explored on foot, and the creek and its boats are a short walk away. Check current opening arrangements before you go, and allow an hour or so.

Stand in the old fort courtyard, with the desert town's story around you and the modern skyline just beyond the walls, and the speed of Dubai's change becomes real. Few cities have transformed so fast. When you step back out, the towers look different for knowing what came before.
