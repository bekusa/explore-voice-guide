The guide

The story

Slip off your shoes and feel the sand. It is soft and warm, the color of pale gold. The water of the bay rolls in low waves. Far off, the hills meet the sea. A light wind carries salt and sunscreen. Somewhere down the sand, a beach game is already starting. This is Playa de Oro, a long beach in the north hotel zone of Puerto Vallarta.

The name means Golden Beach. It comes from an old hotel that once stood here, called the Playa de Oro Hotel. That hotel was one of the first built in this part of the city. The hotel gave the beach its name, and the name stayed even as the coast filled with new resorts.

The beach lies in the north hotel zone, away from the old town. It sits between the marina and the mouth of the Pitillal River. From the center of Puerto Vallarta, it is about six kilometers north, close to a fifteen minute drive. Taxis and buses run along the main road behind the beach. Large hotels and tall condos line the sand behind it.

Playa de Oro is long. It runs for more than three thousand feet along the shore. The sand is soft and amber under the sun. There is room to walk for a long time. Joggers pass in the morning, and walkers come out when the heat fades. Even when hotels are full, the beach rarely feels tight. You can find an open stretch with little effort.

This beach holds a special mark. It has earned the Blue Flag, an international award. The flag is given to beaches that keep clean water, safe conditions, and good care of the shore. Not every beach earns it. Clean sand and clear water are part of what brings people back. The mark tells you this stretch of sand is looked after with care.

The waves here are gentle. The water of Banderas Bay is calm on most days. That makes it good for swimming and for play in the shallows. Children wade near the edge. The slope into the water is soft. Still, watch the flags and the lifeguards, as the sea can change.

Look out across the water and you will see the shape of the bay. Banderas Bay is wide and curved, one of the largest bays in Mexico. Hills rise green behind the city. On clear days the far shore seems close. Boats move between the marina and the open sea. The view stretches far in every direction.

There is much to do on the sand and water. People play beach volleyball. Some ride horses along the shore at sunset. Out on the bay you may see jet skis, kayaks, and banana boats. Parasails rise behind the boats. If you want action, this beach can give it.

You can also do nothing at all. Lay out a towel. Rent a chair from a hotel or a vendor. Watch the boats cross the bay. The beach is wide enough to hold both the busy and the still. Time moves slowly here, measured only by the tide and the sun. Many people come simply to walk the shore and let the day pass.

Vendors walk the sand through the day. They carry fruit, drinks, and crafts. Some sell silver, hats, or blankets. A wave of the hand turns them away with no trouble. A nod brings them over. Most carry a price in mind, so a calm question about cost helps.

Getting here is simple. The beach has good public access between the hotels. By law, all beaches in Mexico are open to everyone. You can reach the sand through walkways near the resorts. Walkways and signs make it easy to find your way back. There are shops, food stores, and restaurants close by, so you can find what you need.

Stay for the end of the day. Puerto Vallarta faces west, out over the water. The sun sets straight into the bay. The sky turns orange, then pink, then deep blue. People stop walking to watch. It is one of the quiet rewards of a day spent here. The gold sand takes on the color of the sky for a few short minutes.

As the light fades, the beach grows quiet. The waves keep their slow beat. The hills go dark against the last of the sun. Playa de Oro asks nothing from you but your time. Stand at the water's edge, let it touch your feet, and watch the bay hold the last of the day.
