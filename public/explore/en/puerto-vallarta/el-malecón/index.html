The story

In the evening, the boardwalk fills with the sound of footsteps and distant music. Waves slap against the sea wall below. The air is warm and smells of salt and grilled corn. To the west, the sun drops slowly toward the wide bay. This is the Malecon, the long seaside walk at the heart of Puerto Vallarta.

The Malecon runs for about a mile along the edge of the old town. On one side is the open water of Banderas Bay. On the other side are shops, cafes, and restaurants. The walkway is paved with smooth stone. There are no cars here. People come on foot, alone and in groups, to walk slowly beside the sea.

For much of the day, the boardwalk is calm and open to the sun. By late afternoon it begins to fill. Families arrive with children in hand. Street vendors set up their carts and light their grills. Musicians find their corners and begin to tune. As the sun goes down, the whole length of the walk comes alive with movement, color, and sound.

The Malecon is known for its many sculptures. They stand in a line along the seaward edge. Some are realistic and easy to read. Others are strange and dreamlike, full of odd shapes and figures. Together they form a kind of open-air gallery that anyone can visit for free, at any hour of the day.

The most loved of these sculptures is The Boy on the Seahorse. It shows a young boy seated on a rearing seahorse, one arm raised. It was made by the Mexican artist Francisco Rafael Zamarripa Castaneda. The figure has become a symbol of the whole town. You will see its shape on signs and souvenirs across Puerto Vallarta.

The story of this sculpture is longer than it looks. An earlier version once stood on a rock at Los Muertos Beach, just to the south. A strong storm swept it out to sea, and for years it was gone. A larger copy, about three meters tall, was made in 1976 and placed here on the Malecon. It was among the first sculptures to stand on this walk.

The lost original was later found in the sea and brought back. It was carefully restored and returned to its place near Los Muertos Beach. So today there are two seahorses in Puerto Vallarta. One stands quietly by the southern beach. The other rides here on the Malecon, one arm raised, watching over the wide bay.

In 2011, the whole boardwalk was rebuilt. Cars were removed, and the walkway was paved fresh in stone. The sculptures were arranged with more care. As part of that work, the seahorse was moved a short way to where two streets, Morelos and Iturbide, meet the sea. It has stood at that crossing ever since.

Since 2018, the seahorse has shared its corner with large bright letters that spell out the name of the city. In the evening the letters glow. People line up to take photographs in front of them, with the dark bay behind. It has become one of the most popular places to stand on the whole walk.

As you move along the Malecon, watch the other sculptures too. Some show human figures stretched and twisted in unusual ways. Others mix people with ladders, pillows, and sea creatures. Each one has its own artist and its own idea. Stopping to read about them slows your walk in a pleasant way.

Look out over the sea wall to the bay itself. Banderas Bay is one of the largest bays in Mexico. Its water can turn silver, gold, or deep blue depending on the hour. Pelicans glide low over the surface, then drop fast to fish. Small boats cross in the distance, trailing thin white wakes. At sunset the sky behind them fills with slow color.

The Malecon is also a stage for street life. You may pass clowns, dancers, mimes painted to look like statues, and musicians playing for the crowd. Performers sometimes build towers of stones that seem to balance against all reason. The walk is never quite the same from one evening to the next.

Come here as the day ends. Find a spot along the rail. Let the crowd move past you while the sun sinks into the bay. The Malecon holds the ordinary life of the town and the wide patience of the sea, side by side, in the soft light of evening.

Key facts

The Boy on the Seahorse was made by Zamarripa Castaneda.
This three-meter seahorse replica dates from 1976.
It was among the first sculptures on the Malecon.
The boardwalk was rebuilt and pedestrianized in 2011.
Glowing city-name letters were added beside it in 2018.

What to look for

The boy raising one arm on his seahorse.
Pelicans gliding low over Banderas Bay.
Stone-tower balancers performing along the rail.
Twisted human figures among the bronze sculptures.

Practical tips

Walk the boardwalk at sunset for the best light.
Stop to read each sculpture's artist and title.
Photograph the lit city letters after dark.
Bring small cash for street vendors and performers.
