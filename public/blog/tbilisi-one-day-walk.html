Tbilisi in One Day: A Self-Guided Walk Through the Old Town

Last verified October 20268 min readLokali Journal
Short answer

One day is enough for Tbilisi's Old Town on foot. Ride the cable car up to Narikala Fortress first, walk down through Abanotubani's sulphur bath district, cross at Metekhi Church, then follow the river to Sioni Cathedral and the Peace Bridge. Finish with a private bathhouse room.

Tbilisi's Old Town is compact, vertical and entirely walkable in a day. The city sits in a river gorge with a fortress on the ridge above it, which gives you a simple and very effective plan: get to the top first by cable car, then spend the day walking downhill.

The name of the city comes from the Georgian word for warm - tbili - after the sulphur springs the old town is built on. Those springs are still running under the brick domes of Abanotubani, and a bathhouse at the end of a long walking day is the correct way to finish this route, not an afterthought.

Morning - up and over

  1. 9:30
    Narikala Fortress
    Take the aerial cable car up from Rike Park rather than walking - it is a short ride, costs very little on a transport card, and saves your legs a steep forty minutes. Fourth-century foundations, and the best single view of the city. Allow an hour including the walk along the ridge.
  2. 10:45
    Kartlis Deda and the ridge walk
    The aluminium Mother of Georgia statue holds a sword in one hand and a wine bowl in the other - for enemies and for guests respectively, which is a fair summary of the national self-image. Walk the ridge path, then descend through the switchbacks towards the baths.
  3. 11:45
    Abanotubani sulphur bath district
    The brick domes at ground level are the roofs of the bathhouses, half-sunk into the hillside. Look at them now and come back at the end of the day - book a private room for later while you are here.

Midday - the river and the churches

  1. 12:30
    Lunch on Leselidze Street
    The Old Town's main artery, properly named Kote Abkhazi. Order khinkali - twist off the top knot, do not eat it, and do not use a fork.
  2. 14:00
    Sioni Cathedral
    The city's cathedral for most of the last thousand years. Dark, candle-lit and still very much in use. Cover shoulders; women are generally expected to cover their hair, and wrap skirts are available at the door.
  3. 14:45
    Anchiskhati Basilica
    Two minutes away and the oldest surviving church in the city, sixth century. Small, plain, and a complete contrast to Sioni. If there is a choir rehearsing, stay.
  4. 15:30
    Metekhi Church
    Cross the river and climb the short rise to the church on the cliff above it, with King Vakhtang Gorgasali on horseback outside. This is the view of Old Tbilisi that ends up on every postcard, looking back the way you came.
  5. 16:30
    Peace Bridge
    The glass-and-steel footbridge from 2010. Locals were not initially kind about it, and it has since become one of the images of the city. Cross it into Rike Park.

Evening - the baths and dinner

Go back to Abanotubani for the room you booked in the morning. Private rooms are rented by the hour for a group rather than by the person, and they include a hot sulphur pool and usually a cold plunge. The water smells of sulphur - that is the point, and it fades from your skin within the hour. A kisi scrub from an attendant is worth adding; it is more vigorous than most first-timers expect.

For dinner, walk up into Sololaki, the district of carved wooden balconies and crumbling art-nouveau stairwells immediately west of the Old Town. If you have energy afterwards, Shardeni Street is the Old Town's bar lane, and the opera house on Rustaveli Avenue is worth checking for the evening's programme - tickets cost a fraction of what the equivalent would in western Europe.

If you have a second day

Leave the city. Mtskheta is twenty minutes away, was Georgia's capital for roughly eight hundred years, and holds the cathedral where the country's kings were crowned - with Jvari Monastery on the hill above the confluence of two rivers. Both are UNESCO-listed and both are a straightforward half-day by marshrutka or taxi. For something harder, David Gareja is a cave monastery cut into a desert ridge on the Azerbaijani border, about two hours out.

Practical notes

  • Get a Metromoney card at any metro station. It covers the metro, buses and the Narikala cable car.
  • The Old Town's pavements are uneven and the hills are real. This route is about seven kilometres with a lot of vertical.
  • Book a bathhouse room in the morning for the evening, especially at weekends. The good rooms go.
  • Carry some cash. Cards are widely accepted, but bathhouses, marshrutkas and small bakeries often are not.
  • Georgian has its own alphabet and street signs are not always transliterated. Download the offline map before you start walking.

Frequently asked

Is one day enough to see Tbilisi?

One day is enough for the Old Town, which is where almost everything a first-time visitor wants to see is concentrated. The route above covers the fortress, the bath district, three historic churches, the river crossings and a bathhouse in roughly seven kilometres of walking. What one day cannot include is Mtskheta, the museums on Rustaveli Avenue, the Soviet-era architecture, the flea market at the Dry Bridge, or any of the wine regions. Two days in the city plus a half-day in Mtskheta is the version most visitors wish they had planned. If a single day is genuinely all you have, protect two things in it: the cable car up to Narikala in the morning, when the light across the gorge is best, and a booked bathhouse room in the evening. Everything between those two is flexible.

Should I walk up to Narikala Fortress or take the cable car?

Take the cable car up and walk down. The ascent on foot is steep, largely unshaded and takes around forty minutes from the Old Town; the aerial cable car from Rike Park takes a couple of minutes, costs very little on a Metromoney card, and gives you the gorge from above on the way. Walking down through the switchbacks towards Abanotubani is the pleasant direction, and it drops you exactly where the next stop on this route is. If you want the walk up, do it early in the morning before the heat, and take the path through the botanical garden rather than the road. One caveat: the cable car does not run in high wind and occasionally closes for maintenance, so have the walk as a fallback rather than discovering the closure with a full day planned around it.

What are the Tbilisi sulphur baths actually like?

The water comes out of the ground hot and smelling strongly of sulphur, and the bathhouses in Abanotubani are built directly over the springs - the brick domes at street level are their roofs. Most visitors rent a private room by the hour for their group rather than using a public hall: you get a hot sulphur pool, usually a cold plunge and a shower, and complete privacy. An attendant scrub is worth adding and is more thorough than most people expect. Bring a towel or pay for one, bring flip-flops if you have them, book ahead at weekends, and do not plan anything strenuous afterwards.

Is Tbilisi expensive for tourists?

By western European or North American standards, no - it is one of the better value city breaks in the region, and that holds across accommodation, restaurants, transport and wine. Public transport and the cable car cost very little, museum entry is modest, and a full dinner with wine in the Old Town is a fraction of the equivalent in Rome or Istanbul. Prices in the most touristed lanes of the Old Town run noticeably higher than two streets away, which is the usual pattern. Carry some cash: cards are common but bathhouses, minibuses and small bakeries frequently are not.
